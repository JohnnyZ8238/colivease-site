How to Rent With No Credit History in Canada (2026)

How to rent with no credit history in Canada: what landlords can ask, documents that replace a credit score, guarantors, Ontario deposit rules and more.

Updated October 31, 2026 · 8 min read · ColivEase

You can rent in Canada with no credit history by replacing the credit report with other proof that you'll pay on time: an employment or offer letter, recent bank statements, references from past landlords (even abroad) and, if needed, a guarantor. In Ontario, landlords may ask for credit information, but the Ontario Human Rights Commission says a lack of credit history shouldn't count against you, and a landlord can't require more than a last month's rent deposit and a refundable key deposit.

Key takeaways

  • No credit history is not bad credit. Ontario's human rights guidance says landlords shouldn't treat a lack of history negatively.
  • Build a "proof package" of income, savings, references and ID to answer the landlord's real question: will you pay?
  • A guarantor is allowed. Ontario's regulations let landlords require a guarantee for the rent.
  • Deposits are capped. A landlord can't require more than one month's rent as a deposit (applied to your last month) plus a refundable key deposit.
  • You don't need to give your SIN to rent a home, and you should be careful who gets copies of your passport.
  • Start building credit right away with a bank account and a credit card you pay off in full.

What landlords usually ask for

Most Toronto landlords and property managers use an application form and ask for some mix of:

  • Photo ID (passport, and your permit or PR documents if you have them)
  • Consent to run a credit check
  • Proof of income: an employment letter, offer letter, recent pay stubs or proof of funds
  • Previous landlord references and rental history
  • Personal or professional references

In Ontario, a regulation under the Human Rights Code spells out what's allowed. Landlords may request credit references, rental history and authorization for a credit check, and they may ask about income, but income information should be considered together with the other information, not on its own. The Ontario Human Rights Commission adds that tribunals and courts have found rigid rent-to-income ratios to be discriminatory.

Build a strong no-credit application

Landlords decide quickly, especially for good units. Have this ready as one clean PDF you can send right after a viewing:

  1. A short cover note introducing you, your job or program, your move-in date and why you're a reliable tenant.
  2. Proof of income or funds: employment or offer letter, two or three recent pay stubs, or bank statements. You can black out account numbers except the last few digits.
  3. References: previous landlord, employer and one personal reference.
  4. ID: a copy of your photo ID, shared only after you've verified the landlord is real.
  5. Credit check consent or a self-pulled report once you have Canadian credit.
  6. Guarantor details, if you have one lined up.

Keep it honest. Overstating income or inventing references can get your lease terminated later.

Guarantors and co-signers

A guarantor signs a separate agreement promising to pay if you don't. Ontario Regulation 290/98 allows landlords to require a guarantee for the rent, so being asked for one is not unusual for newcomers. Landlords typically want a guarantor who lives in Canada and has good credit and steady income, such as a relative, friend or, occasionally, an employer.

Before someone guarantees for you, make sure they understand they could be on the hook for unpaid rent or damage for the whole lease. Some private companies also offer rent guarantee services for a fee. Read the terms carefully and compare the cost with other options.

Prepaid rent: what's allowed in Ontario

This is where newcomers most often get pushed into something they don't have to do.

  • What a landlord can require: a rent deposit of no more than one month's rent (or one rent period, if shorter), which must be used for your last month, plus a refundable key deposit. Damage deposits are not allowed.
  • What a landlord can't require: several months of rent upfront as a condition of renting to you.
  • What you can choose to do: some tenants voluntarily offer extra months of prepaid rent to stand out. A landlord can generally accept prepaid rent that a tenant genuinely volunteers, but this is a grey area: it must be your own idea, not something the landlord demands or hints is required.

If you decide to offer prepaid rent:

  • Only pay after you've verified the landlord, seen the unit and signed the lease. Most Ontario tenancies use the Standard Lease.
  • Put it in writing that the prepayment is voluntary and which months it covers.
  • Pay by a traceable method and get a receipt.
  • Never pay a large sum to someone you haven't verified. "Pay six months now and the place is yours" from a stranger online is a classic scam.

If a landlord insists on more than the legal deposit, you can get advice from the Landlord and Tenant Board or a community legal clinic. More detail is in our guide to rent deposit rules in Ontario.

Protect your personal information

  • SIN: Service Canada lists renting a property and filling out a rental application among situations where you don't need to provide your Social Insurance Number. A credit check can usually be done with your name, date of birth and address.
  • Passport and permit copies: share them only after you've confirmed the landlord or agent is real. Watermark copies with "For rental application at [address] only".
  • Bank details: never share online banking passwords or one-time codes.

Build Canadian credit fast

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada explains that your credit history is kept by Canada's two main credit bureaus, Equifax and TransUnion, and that you can request your credit report from each for free. To get started:

  1. Open a bank account soon after you arrive. Many banks have newcomer packages.
  2. Get a credit card. If you can't qualify for a regular card, a secured card, backed by a deposit you provide, is a common starting point.
  3. Use it lightly and pay the full balance on time, every month. Payment history and keeping your balance low relative to your limit matter a lot.
  4. Check your credit reports after a few months to make sure your accounts appear and the details are correct.
  5. Be patient. A score usually appears after several months of activity and grows stronger with time.

Stay safe while you search

Scammers target newcomers precisely because they know you lack credit history and may feel desperate. Watch for listings that promise "no credit check, just send a deposit", landlords who can't show the unit, and pressure to pay before signing anything. If you're targeted, report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre and your local police.

Frequently asked questions

Can a landlord refuse me just because I have no credit history?

In Ontario, landlords may ask for credit references, rental history and a credit check, but the Ontario Human Rights Commission says a lack of credit or rental history should not be held against you. A landlord who treats having no history as a bad history may be acting in a discriminatory way. You can contact the Human Rights Legal Support Centre if you think this happened to you.

Do I have to give my SIN to a landlord?

No. Service Canada lists renting a property and filling out a rental application among the situations where you don't need to provide your Social Insurance Number. A landlord can usually run a credit check using your name, date of birth and address instead.

Can a landlord in Ontario ask for 6 or 12 months' rent upfront?

A landlord in Ontario can't require more than a rent deposit of up to one month's rent, used for your last month, plus a refundable key deposit. Some tenants choose to offer prepaid rent to strengthen an application, but that must be your own voluntary decision, never a condition of renting. If you're pressured, get advice from the Landlord and Tenant Board or a legal clinic.

How long does it take to build a credit score in Canada?

It usually takes several months of regular, on-time activity on a credit product, such as a credit card, before a score appears, and longer to build a strong one. Paying your full balance on time every month and keeping your balance low are the habits that matter most.

What is a rent guarantor and who can be one?

A guarantor signs an agreement promising to pay your rent if you don't. Ontario's human rights regulations allow landlords to require a guarantee. Landlords usually want someone who lives in Canada with good credit and stable income, such as a relative, friend or employer.
