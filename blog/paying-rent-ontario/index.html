Paying Rent in Ontario: Receipts, Cheques and Late Rent

Paying rent in Ontario: due dates, free rent receipts, post-dated cheques and auto-pay rules, e-transfers, late rent and the 7-day N4, and deposit interest.

Updated November 3, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

In Ontario, rent is due in full on the date set in your lease. Your landlord must give you a free receipt whenever you ask, and can't require post-dated cheques or automatic payments. Late fees aren't allowed, but if rent is even a day late a landlord can serve an N4 notice, which now gives you 7 days to pay before they can apply to the Landlord and Tenant Board (LTB).

Here's how each rule works, plus how to pay safely by e-transfer and what happens to the interest on your last month's rent.

Key takeaways

  • Due date: whatever your lease says. There's no legal grace period.
  • Receipts: free on request, for rent, deposits and other charges, and for up to 12 months after you move out.
  • Post-dated cheques and auto-pay: optional. A landlord can't refuse to rent to you or evict you for declining.
  • No late fees or interest on arrears. A bounced payment can cost the bank's NSF fee plus up to $20.
  • N4 notice period: 7 days for notices given on or after September 21, 2026.
  • Deposit interest: 2.1% due in 2026, 1.9% due in 2027.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your situation, contact the Landlord and Tenant Board or a community legal clinic through Legal Aid Ontario or Steps to Justice.

When rent is due

Your lease sets the due date and how rent is paid. On the Ontario Standard Lease, section 5 lists the total rent, the day it's due, who to pay, the payment methods and any administration charge for cheques returned by a bank.

A few things to know:

  • Pay the full amount on time. If the full rent isn't paid on the day it's due, the landlord can serve an N4. Partial payments still leave you in arrears.
  • Build in a buffer. If the due date falls on a weekend or holiday, send your payment a few days early so it clearly arrives on time.
  • Roommates on one lease are each responsible for the full rent, not just their share. If one person doesn't pay, the landlord can look to all of you. Our guide to splitting rent fairly covers how to set this up between you.

Rent receipts: free, on request

Under the Residential Tenancies Act (RTA), your landlord must give you a receipt for any rent payment, rent deposit or other charge if you ask. They can't charge for it. Former tenants can ask for one within 12 months after the tenancy ends. Refusing to give a receipt is an offence that you can report to the province's Rental Housing Enforcement Unit.

Ask for receipts even if you pay electronically. A good receipt shows:

  • the date and amount paid
  • the rental period it covers (for example, "rent for December 2026")
  • the rental unit address
  • the landlord's name and signature (or email confirmation)

Keep them in one folder. They're your proof if there's ever a dispute about what you paid.

Post-dated cheques and automatic payments

A landlord can't require you to pay by:

  • post-dated cheques
  • pre-authorized debit from your bank account
  • automatic credit card charges, or any other automatic payment

They can suggest these methods, and you can agree if you want to. But a landlord can't refuse to rent to you, or try to evict you, because you said no. This is true even if a lease clause says otherwise.

Once a method is agreed, it stays unless you both agree to change it. If your landlord suddenly demands a new app or method, you don't have to switch. If you do agree to change, put it in writing or keep the text or email.

Paying by e-transfer

E-transfer is the most common method in Toronto. To use it safely:

  • Confirm the recipient. The name shown when you add the payee should match the landlord or property manager on your lease.
  • Use the message field. Write the address and period, for example "Unit 3, 123 Example St: rent Dec 2026".
  • Save confirmations. Screenshot or export each one; your bank may not keep history forever.
  • Never send money before you've signed a lease with a verified landlord. Requests to e-transfer a deposit to hold a unit you haven't seen are a classic scam. See Toronto rental scams.
  • Ask for a receipt anyway. An e-transfer record proves money moved; a receipt confirms what it was for.

If you pay cash, always get a receipt on the spot.

Late rent: what landlords can and can't do

No late fees or interest

Ontario landlords can't charge late fees or interest on unpaid rent, and lease terms requiring them are void. What a landlord can recover:

  • the NSF fee their bank charged if your cheque bounced or payment failed
  • an administration charge of up to $20 for that returned payment
  • the LTB filing fee, if they apply and the LTB orders you to pay it

Landlords can offer a discount for paying on time. If your lease has one, you'll owe the full, undiscounted rent if you're late.

The N4 notice

If rent is late, the landlord can give you Form N4: Notice to End your Tenancy Early for Non-payment of Rent.

  • For an N4 given on or after September 21, 2026, the termination date must be at least 7 days after the notice is given (it used to be 14 days for monthly tenancies).
  • You can void it. Pay all the arrears in the notice, plus any rent that has come due since, before the landlord files an application, and the notice is cancelled. Get a receipt.
  • You don't have to move out because of an N4. The landlord can apply to the LTB (Form L1) starting the day after the termination date, and only an LTB order enforced by the Sheriff can evict you.

If the landlord applies to the LTB

  • You can pay what's owed (including the filing fee) before the order is issued, work out a payment plan using the LTB's mandatory Payment Agreement Form, or go to the hearing.
  • New in 2026: for applications filed on or after September 21, 2026, if you want to raise other issues at the hearing, such as repairs, you must pay the landlord at least half of the arrears claimed at least seven days before the hearing, and give written notice of the issues. Pay the landlord directly and keep a receipt.
  • Persistent late payment now has a definition: rent received more than seven days after the due date at least three times within six months. That matters because persistent late payment is one of the reasons a landlord can give for trying to end a tenancy.
  • Unpaid rent can also be reported to a credit bureau, which can make future rentals harder.

If you're struggling, talk to your landlord early and in writing, and contact a legal clinic. Withholding rent because of repair problems is risky; the LTB has tenant applications for that. Our sibling guides on getting repairs done and LTB tenant applications explain the safer route.

Your last month's rent deposit and its interest

If you paid a rent deposit (usually called last month's rent), your landlord must:

  • use it only for your last rent period, not for damage or cleaning
  • pay you interest every year at the rent increase guideline rate in effect when the interest is due

Example: you've paid a $2,000 deposit. When a year's interest falls due in 2026, it's $2,000 × 2.1% = $42.

When rent goes up, the landlord can ask you to increase the deposit by the same amount, and often uses the interest to do it, so the deposit keeps matching one month's rent. If your rent doesn't go up, the interest should be paid to you or credited against rent. Not paying deposit interest is an offence under the RTA; you can ask in writing and, if needed, apply to the LTB with Form T1.

Rent payment checklist

  • Lease section 5 shows the amount, due date and payment method
  • You chose the method; nobody required cheques or auto-pay
  • Every payment has a receipt or saved confirmation
  • E-transfers go to the name on the lease, with a clear memo
  • No late fees or interest have been charged
  • Deposit interest has been paid or added each year
  • Roommates know the full rent is a shared responsibility

Bottom line

Pay on time, pay in a way you chose and keep proof of every payment. If you fall behind, know that an N4 now gives you just 7 days, that you can void it by paying everything owed, and that only the LTB can end your tenancy.

Frequently asked questions

Does my landlord have to give me a rent receipt in Ontario?

Yes, if you ask. A landlord must give a free receipt for rent, a rent deposit or any other charge you pay. Former tenants can also ask for one within 12 months after the tenancy ends. Charging for a receipt or refusing to give one is an offence under the Residential Tenancies Act.

Can my landlord make me give post-dated cheques?

No. A landlord can suggest post-dated cheques or automatic payments such as pre-authorized debits or credit card charges, but can't require them, refuse to rent to you or evict you for saying no. Once you and the landlord agree on a payment method, it can only change if you both agree.

Can a landlord charge a late fee for rent in Ontario?

No. Landlords can't charge late fees or interest on unpaid rent. If a cheque bounces or a payment fails, you may have to cover the bank's NSF fee plus an administration charge of up to $20. Discounts for paying on time are allowed, so read any discount terms in your lease carefully.

How long does an N4 give me to pay late rent?

For an N4 given on or after September 21, 2026, the termination date must be at least 7 days after the notice is given. If you pay all the rent owing, plus any rent that has since come due, before the landlord files an eviction application, the notice is void and you don't have to move.

Do I get interest on my last month's rent deposit?

Yes. Your landlord must pay interest on the rent deposit every year at the rent increase guideline rate in effect when it's due: 2.1% for interest due in 2026 and 1.9% for 2027. The landlord can pay it to you or use it to top up the deposit after a rent increase.
