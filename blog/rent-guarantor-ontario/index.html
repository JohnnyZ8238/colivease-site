Rent Guarantor in Ontario: Rules, Risks and Alternatives

Need a rent guarantor in Ontario? What a guarantor is liable for, when landlords can ask for one, co-signer vs guarantor, and what to do if you don't have one.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

A rent guarantor in Ontario is someone who signs an agreement promising to pay your rent, and sometimes other costs, if you don't. Ontario's human rights regulations allow landlords to require a guarantee for the rent, so being asked for one is common for students, newcomers and anyone without Canadian credit or income history. If you don't have a guarantor, you can still rent by offering other proof that you'll pay, such as bank statements, an employment letter or strong references.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your situation, contact the Landlord and Tenant Board or a community legal clinic.

Key takeaways

  • It's allowed. Ontario Regulation 290/98 lets landlords require a guarantee for the rent.
  • It should apply equally. The Ontario Human Rights Commission says a guarantor requirement should apply to all tenants, not just newcomers or other protected groups.
  • A guarantor isn't a tenant. They promise to pay but have no right to live in the unit.
  • The agreement decides the risk. What the guarantor covers, and for how long, depends on the wording.
  • No guarantor? You have options: proof of funds, references, a roommate with established credit, or a guarantor service.

What a rent guarantor is liable for

A guarantee is a separate promise to the landlord. The Ontario Standard Lease doesn't have a dedicated guarantor section, so guarantees are usually a separate document signed alongside the lease, or written into the additional terms.

Depending on the wording, a guarantor might be responsible for:

  • unpaid rent, the most common and sometimes the only item
  • damage beyond normal wear and tear
  • amounts a tribunal or court orders the tenant to pay, such as rent arrears
  • costs of collecting those amounts, if the agreement says so

If the tenant falls behind, the landlord may ask the guarantor to pay and may take legal action to recover the money. That's why a guarantor should read the agreement as carefully as the tenant reads the lease.

Can a landlord ask for a guarantor in Ontario?

Yes. Under the Human Rights Code, Ontario Regulation 290/98 sets out what landlords may consider when choosing tenants. It allows landlords to ask for credit references, rental history and permission to run a credit check, to consider income information together with other information, and to require a prospective tenant to obtain a guarantee for the rent.

There are limits. The Ontario Human Rights Commission's rental housing policy says landlords can ask for a guarantor only if they have the same requirement for all tenants. Asking only applicants who are recent immigrants, for example, or who receive social assistance, could be discrimination. The Commission also says a lack of Canadian credit or rental history shouldn't be treated as bad credit.

A guarantee also doesn't change Ontario's deposit rules. A landlord still can't require a rent deposit of more than one month's rent (or one rent period if you pay weekly), and any key deposit must be refundable.

Co-signer for an apartment in Canada: guarantor vs co-tenant

People often say "co-signer" and "guarantor" as if they mean the same thing. In practice, it depends on what the person actually signs.

GuarantorCo-tenant (signs the lease as a tenant)
What they signA separate guaranteeThe lease itself
Right to live in the unitNoYes
Responsible for rentIf the tenant doesn't pay, as the guarantee saysYes, usually together with the other tenants

Typical exampleA parent backing a studentA roommate, partner or family member who lives there

If a parent "co-signs" by signing the lease as a tenant, they may become a co-tenant, with a tenant's legal responsibilities. If that's not what you intend, ask for a separate guarantee instead.

What's in a guarantor agreement for renting

Before anyone signs, read for these points. Ask the landlord to fix anything unclear in writing.

  • Names and address: the landlord, tenant(s), guarantor and the rental unit
  • What's covered: rent only, or also damage, tribunal or court orders and collection costs
  • A cap: is there a maximum dollar amount, or is it unlimited?
  • How long it lasts: only the first fixed term, or for as long as the tenancy continues? In Ontario, a tenancy normally continues month to month after a fixed term ends unless it's properly ended, so this detail matters.
  • Roommates: if several tenants share one lease, does the guarantor cover only your share or the whole rent?
  • Notice: will the landlord tell the guarantor promptly if rent is late?
  • Changes: does the guarantee survive a rent increase, a new roommate or a lease renewal?

Risks for the guarantor

Guaranteeing a lease is a real financial commitment. A guarantor could end up paying months of rent and costs, and a lawsuit or unpaid judgment could affect their finances. A shared lease with several roommates raises the stakes if the guarantee covers everyone. Guarantors should only sign for an amount they could afford to pay, and keep a copy of everything.

What if I don't have a guarantor?

Plenty of people rent without one. Our pillar guide to renting with no credit history in Canada goes deeper, but here are the main alternatives:

  1. Show proof of funds. Recent bank statements, a scholarship or sponsorship letter, or proof of tuition paid show you can cover the rent.
  2. Show income. An offer letter or employment letter with your salary, plus recent pay stubs if you have them.
  3. Bring references. Previous landlords (even outside Canada), an employer or a professor.
  4. Rent with an established roommate. Joining a lease with someone who has Canadian credit and income can strengthen the application, though you'll usually share responsibility for the rent.
  5. Rent a room rather than a whole unit. Room rentals in shared homes often have simpler applications.
  6. Consider prepaid rent carefully. Some tenants voluntarily offer extra months upfront. A landlord can't require it, and it's a grey area, so make sure it's your own choice and get it in writing.

A strong, organized application helps whatever your situation. See our rental application guide for Toronto for a checklist of documents.

Guarantor services in Canada

Some private companies act as a guarantor for tenants in exchange for a fee. They can help if you have no family in Canada, but read the terms closely:

  • Will the landlord accept it? Not every landlord or property manager does. Ask before you pay.
  • What does it cover? Rent only, or damage too? Is there a cap?
  • What does it cost, and when? Is the fee one-time or recurring, and is any of it refundable if your application is declined?
  • Do you repay them? If the company pays your landlord, the agreement may let it recover that money from you.
  • Is it a real company? Look for a registered business, clear contact details and reviews. Be wary of anyone who appears through a listing and pushes a specific "guarantee service" before you've even seen the unit; that can be part of a scam.

Frequently asked questions

Who can be a rent guarantor in Ontario?

Landlords usually want someone who lives in Canada, has a good Canadian credit history and earns a steady income, such as a parent, relative, close friend or sometimes an employer. There's no legal list of who qualifies; each landlord sets their own standard. Ask early what the landlord needs to see from the guarantor so there are no surprises.

Can a landlord require a guarantor only from international students?

Ontario's rules let landlords require a guarantee, but the Ontario Human Rights Commission says a guarantor requirement should apply to all tenants, not only to people identified by a protected ground. Singling out applicants because of their citizenship or place of origin could be discriminatory. If you think this happened, the Human Rights Legal Support Centre offers free advice.

Can my parents overseas be my guarantor?

Some landlords accept a guarantor outside Canada, but many don't, because it's harder to check their credit and to collect if something goes wrong. If your family is abroad, offer stronger proof of funds alongside their guarantee, such as bank statements, a scholarship letter or a tuition receipt. It's the landlord's decision whether to accept it.

Can a guarantor back out after the lease is signed?

Usually not on their own. A signed guarantee is a contract, and its wording decides when it ends. Some guarantees end with the first lease term; others continue while the tenancy continues month to month. A guarantor who wants out should talk to the landlord and get any release in writing.

Does a guarantor have the right to live in the apartment?

No. A guarantor promises to pay if the tenant doesn't, but isn't a tenant and has no right to move in. Someone who signs the lease as a tenant is different: they're a co-tenant, with a tenant's rights and responsibilities.
