4 Month Sublet Toronto: A Student Guide to Term Housing

Need a 4 month sublet in Toronto? A student guide to summer and co-op term sublets: when to look, subletting your own room, landlord consent and paperwork.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

A 4 month sublet in Toronto is how many students cover a summer, a co-op work term or a semester away: someone moves into a tenant's room for one term while the original tenant stays on the lease. The two rules that matter most are simple: the tenant needs the landlord's written consent, which can't be arbitrarily or unreasonably refused, and everyone should sign a written sublet agreement that spells out dates, rent and the deposit.

This guide is for students on both sides: finding a term sublet, and subletting your own room while you're away. For the legal background on subletting in general, see our pillar guide to sublet rules in Ontario.

Key takeaways

  • Sublet = temporary. The original tenant plans to return and stays responsible to the landlord.
  • Consent first. Ask the landlord in writing before anyone moves in.
  • Put it on paper: dates, rent, deposit, utilities, furniture, keys and house rules.
  • No profit. A tenant can't charge a subtenant more than the lawful rent.
  • Start early. Term sublets line up with school calendars, so plan around the term you need.
  • Under 28 nights is a different category. Very short stays fall under Toronto's short-term rental rules.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your situation, contact the Landlord and Tenant Board or a community legal clinic; many universities also have student legal services.

Timing around school terms

Most Ontario universities and colleges run three terms: September to December, January to April and May to August. That rhythm drives the sublet market:

  • Summer (May to August) is the busiest time, as students head home, travel or start internships.
  • Fall and winter sublets come from exchange students, co-op placements and graduating students.
  • Students often post their rooms a month or two before a term ends, so start watching early and be ready to move quickly when a good match appears.

Line up your dates with your exams, work start date and flights. A sublet that starts on the 1st but a job that starts on the 15th can still be the right choice; just budget for it.

Finding a summer sublet for students

Where to look

Your school's off-campus housing listings, student groups and faculty boards, Facebook groups and roommate apps are the usual starting points. Wherever you look, sublets attract scammers because students are often searching from another city or country.

Questions to ask the tenant

  • Are you on the lease, and has your landlord agreed to this sublet in writing?
  • What's included: furniture, utilities, internet, laundry?
  • Who are the roommates, and what are the house rules?
  • How is the deposit handled, and when exactly will it come back?
  • What happens if you need to come back early, or I need to leave early?

Red flags

  • They won't show the room on a live video call or in person.
  • They want a deposit before you've seen the place or signed anything.
  • They can't show a lease, or the story about the landlord keeps changing.

How to sublet your room for a term

Step 1: Ask your landlord in writing

Send a short email: your dates, the person's name and contact, and a request for consent. Keep the reply. If the landlord refuses without a good reason, the pillar guide explains your options.

Step 2: Talk to your roommates

They'll live with this person for four months. Let them meet the candidate, and agree on how the decision is made.

Step 3: Screen like a landlord would

Ask for ID, school or job details and a reference. A video call plus one in-person meeting goes a long way.

Step 4: Sign a sublet agreement

  • Start and end dates, and that the room must be vacated on the end date
  • Monthly rent (not more than your lawful rent), due date and how it's paid
  • Deposit amount, what it covers and when it's returned
  • Utilities and internet: included, or split how
  • Furniture and items left behind, with photos
  • Keys and fobs, and when they're returned
  • House rules: guests, quiet hours, cleaning
  • What happens if either side needs to end early

Step 5: Hand over, and stay reachable

Take dated photos of the room, give the subtenant your landlord's contact for urgent repairs, and keep an eye on rent payments. You're still the tenant, so a missed payment is your problem too.

The last week of the term

For subtenants, moving out cleanly is how you get your deposit back. Agree on a walk-through time a few days before the end date, clean to the standard you found, return every key and fob, and take dated photos as you leave. For tenants coming back, check the room against your move-in photos and return the deposit on the date in your agreement, with a short written note of any deductions. Settling up on time keeps the next sublet easy to arrange.

Co-op term housing

Co-op and internship students coming to Toronto for one term usually want furnished, short and close to the office. A sublet often fits best. If you're offered a short fixed-term lease instead, know that in Ontario a fixed-term tenancy doesn't end on its own; to leave on the last day, you'd normally give an N9 at least 60 days before. Signing an N9 at the same time as the lease, as a condition of getting it, is generally void.

If you rent a room in an owner's home and share their kitchen or bathroom, the Residential Tenancies Act usually doesn't apply. That can be fine for one term, but get the dates, the deposit and the notice period in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Can my landlord say no to me subletting my room for the summer?

You need your landlord's consent to sublet, but under section 97 of the Residential Tenancies Act they can't arbitrarily or unreasonably refuse it. A specific concern about the person you propose can be reasonable; a blanket 'never' usually isn't. If you share a kitchen or bathroom with a live-in owner, the RTA generally doesn't apply, so your agreement with the owner decides.

Am I still responsible for rent while someone sublets my room?

Yes. In a sublet you stay the tenant, so if your subtenant doesn't pay or damages the place, the landlord can look to you. Collect rent on a schedule that gives you time to cover a gap, and keep a written record of every payment.

Can I charge more for my sublet than I pay?

No. The RTA doesn't allow a tenant to charge a subtenant more than the lawful rent. Charging your share for your room, plus any utilities you've agreed to split, keeps you on the right side of the rules.

Is a sublet of a few weeks treated differently in Toronto?

Yes. Rentals of less than 28 consecutive nights fall under the City of Toronto's short-term rental rules, which come with registration and principal-residence requirements. A full four-month term is well beyond that threshold.

What if my subtenant won't leave at the end of the term?

A subtenant has no right to stay past the agreed end date. The Residential Tenancies Act gives the tenant and the landlord a way to apply to the Landlord and Tenant Board about an overholding subtenant, and there are time limits, so act quickly and get advice from a legal clinic.
