Renoviction Bylaw Toronto: What the Licence Means for You

Renoviction bylaw Toronto rules as of November 2026: when a landlord needs a City licence for N13 renovations, what tenants get, and Mississauga's new rules.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

Toronto's renoviction bylaw, the Rental Renovation Licence Bylaw, means a landlord who serves an N13 to renovate a unit must apply for a City licence within 7 days, and must house or compensate the tenant as a condition of that licence. It has been in effect since July 31, 2025, and as of November 2026 Mississauga has a similar bylaw, in force since September 1, 2026. Neither bylaw replaces your provincial rights: an N13 is still only a notice, and only the Landlord and Tenant Board (LTB) can order an eviction.

This is general information, not legal advice. For help, call 311, contact a community legal clinic or check with the LTB.

Key takeaways

  • Toronto: landlords must apply for a Rental Renovation Licence within 7 days of serving an N13 for renovations or repairs that need the unit empty.
  • Licence fee: $728 per unit in 2026, according to the City.
  • Tenants who plan to return get temporary housing or monthly rent-gap payments, plus a one-time moving allowance.
  • Tenants who don't return get a lump sum equal to three months of rent-gap payments, plus the moving allowance.
  • Mississauga: a similar licence applies to N13s served on or after September 1, 2026, with a moving contribution of $1,000 or $1,500 depending on unit size.

What is a renoviction?

A "renoviction" is when a landlord uses renovations as a reason to remove a tenant, sometimes to re-rent the unit at a much higher price. Ontario law allows landlords to end a tenancy for genuine work that requires the unit to be empty, using Form N13. The trouble is that tenants often didn't know their rights, accepted small buyouts, or never got the chance to move back.

Toronto and Mississauga responded with local licensing. The bylaws don't decide evictions; that's still the LTB's job. Instead, they add City oversight and set out what the landlord must give you before the work goes ahead.

Renoviction bylaw Toronto: how the licence works

Here's the sequence once a Toronto landlord decides the work requires vacant possession:

  1. The landlord serves an N13 under provincial law.
  2. Within 7 days, the landlord applies to the City for a Rental Renovation Licence, with building permits, a copy of the N13 and a report from a qualified person confirming the unit must be empty.
  3. A Tenant Information Notice goes on your door. This starts a 14-day window for you to review your rights, get advice and connect with tenant support before a licence can be issued.
  4. You declare whether you plan to return. The landlord must give you a form for this.
  5. The landlord submits a plan to house you temporarily, pay monthly rent-gap payments, or pay lump-sum compensation, depending on your choice.
  6. If issued, the licence is posted on your door, and the application shows up in the City's public online registry.

Landlords who break the bylaw can face fines of up to $100,000.

What tenants get under Toronto's rental renovation licence

Your choiceWhat the landlord must provide
You plan to move backComparable temporary housing at a similar rent, or monthly rent-gap payments, plus a one-time moving allowance
You won't move backA lump sum equal to three months of rent-gap payments, plus a one-time moving allowance

Rent-gap payments cover the difference between your current rent and an average market rent figure for your area, based on CMHC data, so you can afford a temporary place while the work is done.

Moving allowance: when the bylaw launched, it was $1,500 for a studio or one-bedroom and $2,500 for two bedrooms or more, indexed each year to Toronto's inflation rate. Ask the City for the current figure.

These City amounts sit alongside any compensation you're owed under the Residential Tenancies Act. How the two fit together can be technical, so get advice from a clinic or the City's Tenant Support Program before accepting a final number.

N13 notice tenant rights: the short version

The bylaw adds a layer, but the provincial N13 rules still do the heavy lifting. In brief:

  • Notice: at least 120 days, ending on the last day of a rental period or fixed term.
  • A qualified person's report must come with the N13 confirming the unit has to be empty, or the notice is void.
  • Right of first refusal: to keep your right to move back, tell the landlord in writing before you move out.
  • Back at the same rent: if you return, your rent can't be more than what the landlord could have charged had you never left.
  • You can stay put until the LTB orders otherwise.

For the full process, including compensation amounts and bad-faith claims, see our guide to N12 and N13 evictions in Ontario.

Watch out for "just sign here"

Some landlords offer a cash payment if you sign an N11 (an agreement to end the tenancy). Once signed, it's very hard to undo: you give up your right to move back, and compensation tied to the N13 process may no longer apply. Read every document, take your time and get advice first.

Mississauga renoviction bylaw

Mississauga City Council passed its Rental Repairs and Renovations Licensing By-law on April 1, 2026, and it took effect on September 1, 2026. Key points for tenants:

  • It applies to N13s served on or after September 1, 2026. Earlier notices fall outside it.
  • Landlords must apply for a City licence within 7 days of serving the N13, in addition to the building permit.
  • No eviction or work before the licence. The City says landlords can't remove you and start the repairs or renovations until the licence is issued.
  • Moving contribution: a one-time payment of $1,000 for units of 70 square metres (about 750 sq ft) or less, or $1,500 for larger units, paid at least 15 days before you move out, whether or not you return.
  • Monthly top-up payments if you relocate temporarily and plan to come back.

If you rent elsewhere in the GTA, check with your city: some Ontario municipalities have their own renovation rules, while others rely only on the provincial N13 process.

Checklist: you just got an N13

  • Note the date you received it and the termination date (at least 120 days later).
  • Check for the qualified person's report.
  • In Toronto or Mississauga, look for the City notice on your door within a week or two, and check the City's registry or call 311.
  • Decide whether you want to move back, and if so, say so in writing before you leave.
  • Keep every letter, text and photo of your unit.
  • Don't sign an N11 or buyout without advice.
  • Know your exit options: our pillar guide on ending a tenancy with an N9 explains how to leave early on your own terms, and the Ontario tenant rights guide covers the rest.

Frequently asked questions

Does my landlord need a licence to renovate my unit in Toronto?

Only when the work requires you to leave and the landlord serves an N13 for repairs or renovations. In that case the landlord must apply to the City for a Rental Renovation Licence within 7 days of serving the N13. Ordinary repairs done while you stay in the unit don't need this licence.

How do I check if my landlord has a renovation licence?

Look for the Tenant Information Notice and, later, the licence itself, which must be posted on your unit's door. The City also keeps a public online registry of applications and licences. If you're unsure, call 311 and ask about the Rental Renovation Licence.

Can I refuse to move out for renovations?

An N13 is a notice, not an eviction order. If you don't agree to leave, the landlord must apply to the Landlord and Tenant Board, and only an LTB order enforced by the Sheriff can make you go. Get legal advice before signing anything that ends your tenancy.

Does the renoviction bylaw apply in Markham, Vaughan or Brampton?

Not Toronto's bylaw, which covers the City of Toronto only, and Mississauga's covers Mississauga only. Elsewhere in the GTA, the provincial N13 rules in the Residential Tenancies Act still apply, so check your own municipality for any local licensing.

What if my landlord renovates and re-rents my unit at a higher price?

If you gave written notice that you wanted to move back and the landlord didn't offer you the unit, or charged more than allowed, you may be able to file an application with the Landlord and Tenant Board. Keep copies of all notices and ads, and talk to a legal clinic.
