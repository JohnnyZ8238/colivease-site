Best Websites to Find a Room in Toronto: Honest Guide

The best websites to find a room in Toronto, compared honestly: listing sites, Kijiji vs Facebook Marketplace, student boards, roommate apps and scam risk.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 8 min read · ColivEase

The best websites to find a room in Toronto depend on what you're after: big listing sites like Rentals.ca and Zumper are strongest for whole apartments, Kijiji and Facebook Marketplace have the most rooms in shared houses, and school housing boards and roommate platforms are built for students and people who want to share. Most renters use two or three at once. The real skill is spotting which listings are genuine, because scam risk varies a lot from one platform to the next.

Key takeaways

  • Use a mix: one big listing site, one roommate or student platform, and one community channel.
  • Kijiji and Facebook Marketplace have huge volume but the least verification, so they need the most caution.
  • Student boards such as Places4Students and your school's own service are a good first stop if you're enrolled.
  • Chinese-language channels (51.ca, YorkBBS, Xiaohongshu, WeChat groups) are fast and convenient but carry real scam risk.
  • On any platform: confirm the landlord, view the unit, and never pay before you sign.

The big listing sites

Rentals.ca

Rentals.ca is one of the largest Canadian listing sites, with many purpose-built rental buildings, condos and houses. It also publishes the monthly National Rent Report with Urbanation, which is handy for checking whether an asking rent is reasonable. It's strongest for whole units; if you only want a room, you'll find fewer options.

Zumper and PadMapper

Zumper bought PadMapper in 2016, and both are map-first search tools. They're useful for seeing what's available around a subway line or campus at a glance. As with any aggregator, double-check that a listing is still live before you get attached.

liv.rent

liv.rent says it verifies landlord identity and checks listings before they go live, and lets renters build a reusable profile for applications. That extra layer of checking appeals to cautious renters, though the selection of single rooms is narrower than on the open classifieds.

Kijiji vs Facebook Marketplace rentals

These two carry a large share of Toronto's room listings, especially rooms in houses, basement units and sublets from private landlords. They have a lot in common, but there are differences worth knowing.

Craigslist works much like Kijiji but routes messages through an anonymous email relay, which makes it harder to confirm who you're talking to. Use the same caution.

Student housing boards

If you're enrolled at a college or university, start here. Many Ontario colleges partner with Places4Students, an off-campus housing listing service; Seneca, for example, says it partners with it. Universities such as U of T and York run their own off-campus housing services. Benefits include listings aimed at students, shorter terms that line up with semesters, and sometimes staff who can give advice if a problem comes up. Schools generally don't inspect listings, though, so verify the landlord and the unit just as you would anywhere else.

Roommate apps Toronto renters use

Roommate platforms focus on the people, not just the room. They help when you're new to the city, want to rent a whole house with others, or care about who you'll share a kitchen with.

What to look for in a roommate platform:

  • Matching on lifestyle, not just budget: sleep schedule, cleanliness, guests, smoking, pets
  • Some form of verification for users and landlords
  • Privacy controls, so your photos and details aren't public by default
  • Clear information about the home, including whether the landlord lives there
  • A way to apply as a group if you're renting with friends

Chinese-language channels

Many Chinese-speaking newcomers and students search on 51.ca, YorkBBS (约克论坛), Xiaohongshu (RED) and WeChat groups. They're fast, you can ask questions in your own language, and you'll often find roommates with similar routines. They're also where fake landlords, "second landlords" re-renting without permission and deposit scams cluster, especially in August and December before semesters start. Verify the person's identity on a live video call, confirm they own or manage the unit, and use the Ontario Standard Lease.

How to use any rental site safely

  1. Search the address and photos. Reverse-image search the pictures and look up the address to see if it's listed elsewhere at a different price.
  2. Confirm who you're dealing with. Ask for the landlord's full legal name and check it against the lease. For condos, ask whether they're the owner or a licensed agent.
  3. See it before you pay. View in person, or by live video where the person shows the street, building entrance and unit number.
  4. Sign first, pay second. Most residential tenancies in Ontario use the Ontario Standard Lease. A rent deposit can't be more than one month's rent.
  5. Pay traceably. Use e-transfer or cheque and get a receipt. Avoid cash, crypto, gift cards and wire transfers to strangers.

Frequently asked questions

Which rental site in Toronto has the fewest scams?

No site is scam-free. Platforms that verify landlords or listings, and school housing boards that only accept registered landlords, add a layer of checking, while open classifieds and social media groups let anyone post in minutes. Whatever site you use, confirm who owns or manages the unit, view it in person or by live video, and never pay before signing a lease.

Is Facebook Marketplace safe for renting in Toronto?

It can be used safely, but it's one of the easiest places for fake listings because anyone can create a profile. Check how old the profile is, compare the photos with a reverse image search, and insist on seeing the unit before paying anything. Be especially wary of a landlord who says they're out of the country.

Do I have to pay to use rental websites in Toronto?

Most large listing sites are free for renters to browse and contact landlords, though some offer paid extras. Be cautious if anyone asks you to pay a fee just to see a unit's address or book a viewing. Check each platform's own terms before paying for anything.

What is the best way for students to find housing in Toronto?

Start with your school's off-campus housing service, then widen your search to listing sites and roommate platforms. School boards are built for students and some offer advice if something goes wrong. If you're still overseas, use live video viewings and never send a deposit for a place no one has verified.
