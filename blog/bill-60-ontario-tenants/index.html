Bill 60 Ontario Tenants: What Changed in 2026

Bill 60 Ontario tenants guide, as of November 2026: the 7-day N4 notice, the 15-day LTB review deadline, the half-arrears rule and the new N12 rules.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

Bill 60 gave Ontario tenants a new set of rules in 2026: as of November 2026, the notice period on an N4 for unpaid rent is 7 days instead of 14, you have 15 days instead of 30 to ask the Landlord and Tenant Board (LTB) to review an order, and tenants must pay half the claimed arrears before raising their own issues at a rent hearing. Together with parts of Bill 97, the changes took effect in two stages, on July 1 and September 21, 2026. Your basic protections are still there, though: a notice is not an eviction, and only an LTB order enforced by the Sheriff can make you leave.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your situation, contact the LTB, a community legal clinic or, in Toronto, 311.

Key takeaways

  • N4 notice period: 7 days (down from 14) for notices given on or after September 21, 2026.
  • LTB review deadline: 15 days (down from 30) for orders issued on or after July 1, 2026.
  • Half-arrears rule: to raise maintenance or other issues at a rent-arrears hearing, you must pay the landlord at least half of the arrears claimed, at least 7 days before the hearing, for applications filed on or after September 21, 2026.
  • N12 change: no compensation is owed for a landlord's own-use N12 that gives at least 120 days' notice and is served on or after September 21, 2026.
  • New forms: the N4, N12 and N13 were replaced. Old versions may not be valid.

What is Bill 60?

Bill 60, the Fighting Delays, Building Faster Act, 2025, is a provincial housing and infrastructure law that passed at Queen's Park in November 2025 amid protests from tenant groups. Among other things, it amended Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act (RTA), the law that governs most rentals. Some RTA changes from another law, Bill 97, were brought into force on the same schedule.

The government said the changes would speed up the LTB and encourage more rental housing. Tenant advocates, including the Advocacy Centre for Tenants Ontario, argued they make it faster and easier to evict people who fall behind. Either way, the rules are now law, so it pays to know them.

N4 notice 7 days: what actually happens

An N4 is the form a landlord gives when rent is late. Since September 21, 2026, the termination date on a new N4 can be as early as 7 days after it's given, for monthly and yearly tenancies too.

That's tighter, but the steps that follow are the same:

  1. You get the N4. It's a notice, not an order. You don't have to move.
  2. You can void it by paying. Pay the full amount on the notice, plus any rent that has come due since, by the termination date, and the notice no longer counts. Keep proof of every payment.
  3. If arrears remain, the landlord can file an L1 application with the LTB.
  4. A hearing is scheduled. You can still pay what's owed (including the landlord's filing fee) before an eviction order is issued, or ask for a payment plan.
  5. Only the Sheriff can enforce an eviction order. Your landlord can never change the locks or remove your things on their own.

If you're struggling, talk to your landlord early and put any payment plan in writing. Our guide to Ontario tenant rights explains the wider protections you keep.

The half-arrears rule

Before, a tenant facing an arrears hearing could raise problems like broken heat or harassment and ask the LTB to weigh them. You still can, but for applications filed on or after September 21, 2026, you must pay the landlord at least half of the claimed arrears at least 7 days before the hearing, and give written notice of the issues you want to raise. The payment goes to the landlord, not to the LTB in trust. If repairs are your main concern, filing your own application is often the stronger route; see how to file LTB tenant applications.

LTB review deadline 15 days

If the LTB issues an order you believe contains a serious error, such as a hearing you couldn't attend for a good reason, you can request a review. For orders issued on or after July 1, 2026, the deadline is 15 days from the order date, not 30. The LTB can extend it if it considers that just, but you should never count on that.

A practical tip: the day you receive any LTB order, read it, note the date, and call a legal clinic if you disagree. Two weeks goes quickly.

Changes to N12 own-use evictions

An N12 is used when a landlord, a close family member or a buyer plans to move in. For a landlord's own-use N12 served on or after September 21, 2026, no one month's compensation is owed if the notice gives at least 120 days. Shorter notices still require compensation, and purchaser N12s always do.

There's a new safeguard too. If the person named on the notice doesn't move in within 60 days, the LTB presumes bad faith if you later file a T5 application. The landlord can try to rebut that, but the burden shifts in your favour.

What hasn't changed

  • A notice is never an eviction. Only an LTB order, enforced by the Sheriff, can remove you.
  • The rent increase guideline still applies to most units first occupied before November 15, 2018 (2.1% for 2026).
  • Your notice to leave still works the usual way. Tenants end a tenancy with an N9; see ending a tenancy with an N9.
  • Landlords still need 24 hours' written notice to enter for most reasons.

A quick checklist if you get a notice

  • Check the form's version date and the termination date.
  • Confirm the amount owed against your own records.
  • Pay, or arrange a written payment plan, before the termination date if you can.
  • Open every letter from the LTB and note hearing and review dates.
  • Get free advice from a community legal clinic or Tenant Duty Counsel.

Frequently asked questions

Can my landlord evict me 7 days after I miss rent?

No. Seven days is only the minimum notice period on an N4 for notices given on or after September 21, 2026. If you don't pay, the landlord still has to file with the Landlord and Tenant Board, attend a hearing and get an eviction order, and only the Sheriff can enforce it.

Does Bill 60 change rent control in Ontario?

The tenancy changes now in force focus on notices, hearings, reviews and own-use evictions. The annual rent increase guideline still applies to most units first occupied before November 15, 2018, and it is 2.1% for 2026. Check ontario.ca for the current rules before a renewal.

Do the new rules apply to notices I received before September 21, 2026?

Generally not. The LTB says the 7-day N4 period applies to notices given on or after September 21, 2026, the half-arrears rule applies to applications filed on or after that date, and the N12 change applies to notices served on or after it. Older notices follow the earlier rules.

Can I still get more time after an LTB order?

You can ask, but the window to request a review of an order is now 15 days for orders issued on or after July 1, 2026, and the LTB only extends it when it considers that just. Tenant duty counsel or a community legal clinic can help you act quickly.

Where can I get free help with Bill 60 changes?

Contact your local community legal clinic through Legal Aid Ontario, Tenant Duty Counsel on the day of an LTB hearing, or the Advocacy Centre for Tenants Ontario's resources. In Toronto, call 311 to reach the City's Tenant Support Program.
