Richmond Hill Rentals: 2026 Guide for Renters

Browsing Richmond Hill rentals? Compare areas from Richmond Hill Centre to Oak Ridges, check a basement apartment safely and see where the subway stands.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

Richmond Hill rentals are mostly condos along Yonge Street and Highway 7, basement apartments and rooms in detached houses, and a smaller number of townhouses. The city suits renters who want a quieter suburban base with strong Viva and YRT links to TTC Line 1 at Finch, and it has a large Chinese community. The Yonge North Subway Extension is under construction, but no official opening date has been announced, so choose a home that works with today's transit.

Key takeaways

  • Condos cluster around Richmond Hill Centre (Yonge and Highway 7) and along Yonge Street; rooms and basement suites are spread through the residential neighbourhoods.
  • Yonge Street and Highway 7 are the transit spine, with Viva rapid transit and YRT buses connecting to Finch station.
  • Richmond Hill GO trains run mainly at weekday rush hours, so they work best for a standard office commute.
  • The subway extension is being built, with stations planned near Highway 7 and Highway 407, but don't count on a date.
  • Basement apartments are allowed on many lots, but should be built with permits and to code. Check the safety basics before you sign.
  • Many landlords live on site. Ask whether you'll share a kitchen or bathroom with the owner.

Richmond Hill rentals: what you'll find

Richmond Hill stretches north from Highway 7 and Highway 407 up to Oak Ridges, with Yonge Street running through the middle. The main rental types are:

  • Condos around Richmond Hill Centre and Langstaff, and at points along Yonge Street. Most are rented by individual owners.
  • Basement apartments in detached houses across neighbourhoods like Bayview Hill, Rouge Woods, Jefferson and Oak Ridges.
  • Rooms in houses, often with the owner or other tenants upstairs.
  • Townhouses shared by roommate groups.

Richmond Hill basement apartment: what to check

Basement apartments are one of the most common and affordable Richmond Hill rentals. Under Zoning By-law 143-24, the City permits additional residential units, such as basement suites, on many residential lots, with exceptions for some areas, including floodplain zones. Zoning is only part of the picture, though. A suite should also meet the Ontario Building Code and Fire Code, and creating one generally requires a building permit; the City handles these through its additional residential unit permit process.

When you view a basement, check:

  • A safe second way out, such as a proper egress window or a separate exit.
  • Working smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms, especially near the furnace and bedrooms.
  • Ceiling height, heat and ventilation, including signs of dampness or mould.
  • A separate entrance and lockable door between your unit and the main house.
  • Paperwork. Ask whether the suite was built with a building permit and passed inspection.

Even if a basement wasn't built with permits, the Residential Tenancies Act generally still protects you, unless you share a kitchen or bathroom with the owner. Our full guide to legal basement apartments explains your rights and what to do about safety problems.

Yonge North Subway Extension: where it stands

The Yonge North Subway Extension will carry TTC Line 1 about 8 kilometres north from Finch station. Metrolinx's current plan includes five new stations: Steeles, Clark and Royal Orchard underground, and Bridge and High Tech above ground near Highway 7 and Highway 407. In July 2026, Metrolinx said major construction was underway, including the launch shaft for the tunnel boring machines.

What this means for renters:

  • No official opening date has been announced, so treat the subway as a long-term bonus, not a reason to accept a harder commute today.
  • Expect construction disruption along Yonge Street near Steeles and Clark and around the Highway 7 area, including lane shifts and noise.
  • New condos near future stations may advertise the subway heavily. Judge them on today's transit and price.

Getting around Richmond Hill today

  • Viva rapid transit runs along Yonge Street to Finch station and along Highway 7 east from Richmond Hill Centre toward Markham.
  • YRT local buses cover the rest of the city, with less frequent service in outer neighbourhoods.
  • Richmond Hill GO line stations include Langstaff, Richmond Hill and Gormley, with trains to Union Station at weekday rush hours.
  • One Fare makes transfers between YRT and the TTC free when you pay with PRESTO, debit or credit.

Tips before you sign in Richmond Hill

  • Walk the route. Time the walk from the door to the nearest Viva or frequent YRT stop, and check evening and weekend schedules.
  • Confirm who you're renting from. For a condo, make sure the person is the owner or a registered agent; for a room, ask whether the owner lives in the house.
  • Use the standard lease. Most private rentals in Ontario must use the province's standard lease, rather than a homemade agreement.
  • Keep deposits legal. A landlord can require a rent deposit of no more than one month's rent, used for your last month, plus a refundable key deposit.
  • Get extras in writing. Parking, utilities, internet and furniture should all be listed in the lease or a signed addendum.

Chinese community notes

Richmond Hill has a large Chinese-speaking population, especially around Highway 7 and Leslie, where Times Square and nearby plazas are busy with restaurants and shops. Many room and basement listings are posted in Chinese on WeChat groups, Xiaohongshu (RED), 51.ca and YorkBBS. These are handy for finding Mandarin- or Cantonese-speaking housemates, but scams appear there too. Verify the landlord, see the unit in person or on a live video call, and only pay once you have a written lease. North York is another popular choice for Chinese-speaking renters; see our guide to North York apartments for rent.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your situation, contact the Landlord and Tenant Board or a community legal clinic.

Frequently asked questions

Can I live in Richmond Hill without a car?

Yes, if you live close to Yonge Street or Highway 7, where Viva rapid transit and frequent YRT routes run. Neighbourhoods further east, west or north, such as parts of Oak Ridges, have less frequent buses. Check evening and weekend service for your exact address before signing.

How do I get from Richmond Hill to downtown Toronto?

The most common route is Viva or YRT down Yonge Street to Finch station, then TTC Line 1 south; One Fare makes that transfer free with PRESTO, debit or credit. Richmond Hill GO line trains run to Union Station at weekday rush hours, and driving via Highway 404 or Yonge Street is another option.

Is it common to live with the landlord in Richmond Hill?

Yes. Many rooms and basement suites are in homes where the owner lives. If you share a kitchen or bathroom with the owner, the Residential Tenancies Act usually doesn't apply, so ask about the arrangement and agree on house rules and notice in writing.

Are there Chinese-speaking landlords and roommates in Richmond Hill?

Yes, Richmond Hill has a large Chinese community, especially in the central and southern parts of the city around Highway 7. Many listings are posted in Chinese online. Use the same caution as with any listing: verify the landlord and see the unit before paying.
