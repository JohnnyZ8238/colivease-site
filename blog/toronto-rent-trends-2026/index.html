Toronto Rent Prices 2026: Is Rent Still Going Down?

Toronto rent prices 2026, as of November: what Rentals.ca, liv.rent, TRREB and CMHC show, what a 1-bedroom costs, and the best time of year to sign a lease.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

Toronto rent prices in 2026 have stopped falling: as of November 2026, the latest Rentals.ca and Urbanation report shows average asking rents for Toronto apartments and condos up 1.6% in October from a year earlier, the first annual increase in nearly three years. One-bedroom listings, though, are still a little cheaper than in late 2025 in some data. In short, the big discount window of 2024 and 2025 is narrowing, but renters still have more choice than they did a few years ago.

Key takeaways

  • Toronto's average asking rent rose 1.6% year over year in October 2026 (Rentals.ca and Urbanation, apartments and condos), the first annual increase in nearly three years.
  • The low point came around the turn of the year. The same report series had Toronto at $2,498 in December 2025, the lowest since early 2022, and $2,482 in February 2026.
  • One-bedrooms are roughly flat. liv.rent put an unfurnished one-bedroom in the City of Toronto at $1,976 in November 2026, down 1.64% from a year earlier.
  • Vacancy is higher than it was. CMHC's 2025 survey found Toronto's purpose-built vacancy rate at 3%, the first time at that level since the pandemic.
  • Timing still matters. Late fall and winter usually bring less competition than the summer and back-to-school rush.

Why the gap between $1,976 and $2,273 for a one-bedroom? liv.rent tracks listings on its own platform within the City of Toronto, while TRREB tracks condos leased through realtors across the wider GTA, which skews toward newer buildings. Both are useful; just compare like with like.

Is rent going down in Toronto?

Not much anymore. The steep part of the decline is behind us.

How we got here

From early 2024 into 2026, Toronto asking rents sat below year-earlier levels month after month. By December 2025, the Rentals.ca and Urbanation average had fallen to $2,498, down 5.1% from a year earlier and almost 12% over two years. In March 2026, Rentals.ca pointed to a surge of new supply, with new condos and purpose-built rental buildings finishing at the same time, as the main driver. Slower population growth, fewer international students and a shaky job market also cut demand.

What changed this fall

The October 2026 data marked a turn. Toronto's average asking rent rose 1.6% year over year, and three-bedroom units rose faster, up 4.3% to $3,643. Urbanation's president, Shaun Hildebrand, said the window for cheaper rents in Toronto and Vancouver is closing, linking it to fewer new units coming on stream. Nationally, rents were still down 3.2% to $2,036, so Toronto is now moving differently from much of Canada.

What it means for you

  • Moving soon? You can still find move-in incentives in some new buildings, but don't assume prices will keep dropping if you wait.
  • Renewing? Market rents don't change your current lease. For most units first occupied before November 15, 2018, your increase is capped by the 2026 rent increase guideline of 2.1%.
  • Bigger units are firming up first. If you were hoping to share a three-bedroom, start looking early.

Average rent for a 1-bedroom in Toronto

Depending on the source, a one-bedroom in Toronto typically lists for about $2,000 to $2,300 a month as of late 2026:

  • About $1,976 for an unfurnished one-bedroom in the City of Toronto (liv.rent, November 2026). Furnished one-bedrooms averaged $1,931 in the same report.
  • About $2,273 for a one-bedroom condo leased through MLS across the GTA (TRREB, Q2 2026).

Downtown, newer condos and units with parking or in-suite laundry sit at the top of that range. Older low-rise buildings and units further from the core sit lower. Best time of year to rent in Toronto

Toronto's rental market has a clear rhythm, even in a softer year.

Busy season: May to September

Graduates, students and job movers all hunt at once, so good units go fast. Rentals.ca noted in March 2026 that the summer season usually lifts prices, even if it didn't in 2025. Expect more competition, quicker decisions and fewer concessions.

Quieter season: November to February

Fewer people move in winter, so landlords with empty units are often more flexible on price, move-in date or extras like parking. The trade-off is fewer listings to choose from, and moving in snow is no fun.

A simple timing checklist

  • Start looking 6 to 8 weeks before your move date.
  • Compare the asking rent with at least two reports from the same month.
  • Ask whether rent is negotiable, or whether the landlord offers a free month or reduced first month.
  • Check whether the unit is covered by rent control before you sign.
  • Budget for first month's rent plus a last month's deposit.

What to watch over the next few months

Rent data moves quickly, so here's when to check back:

  • Early each month: Rentals.ca and Urbanation publish the National Rent Report, usually in the first week, covering the previous month. This is the quickest read on whether Toronto's October uptick holds.
  • Mid-month: liv.rent's Ontario report adds a one-bedroom view across about 20 Ontario cities and areas, from Oakville to Oshawa.
  • December: CMHC usually releases its annual Rental Market Report, with updated vacancy rates for purpose-built rentals.
  • By August 31: Ontario announces the next year's rent increase guideline, which sets the cap on most renewals for older units.

If several sources point the same way for two or three months in a row, that's a trend. A single month's jump or dip is often just noise.

How to use these numbers without getting burned

Averages are a starting point, not a price tag. A listing far below the numbers above, especially one where the "landlord" can't show the unit in person, is a classic scam pattern. Frequently asked questions

Will Toronto rents go up in 2027?

Nobody can say for sure. As of the October 2026 data, Urbanation's president said the window for cheaper rents in Toronto is closing as fewer new units arrive, while CMHC expected vacancy to stay near or slightly above 3% through 2026. That points to a flatter market rather than the steady declines of 2024 and 2025.

Why did Toronto rents fall in 2024 and 2025?

Analysts point mainly to a wave of new condo and purpose-built rental supply arriving at the same time as slower population growth, fewer international students and a softer job market. More choice gave renters leverage, and asking rents fell for close to three years.

Does a lower asking rent affect my current lease?

Not directly. Your rent only changes at renewal, and for most units first occupied before November 15, 2018, increases are capped by Ontario's guideline (2.1% for 2026). Falling market rents mainly help people who are moving or signing a new lease.

Is it cheaper to rent a room than a 1-bedroom in Toronto?

Almost always. A one-bedroom typically lists for roughly $2,000 to $2,300 depending on the source, while a room in a shared home usually costs far less. Splitting a two-bedroom with a roommate often lands between the two.

Where do Toronto rent numbers come from?

The most quoted are the monthly Rentals.ca and Urbanation National Rent Report, liv.rent's monthly reports, TRREB's quarterly condo rental report and CMHC's annual Rental Market Report. Each measures something different, so always check which source and month a figure comes from.
