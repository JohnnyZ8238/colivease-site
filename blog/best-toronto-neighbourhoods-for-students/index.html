Best Toronto Neighbourhoods for Students (2026 Guide)

The best neighbourhoods in Toronto for students, campus by campus: U of T, TMU, York, OCAD and the colleges, with TTC commutes, vibe and what to watch for.

Updated October 31, 2026 · 8 min read · ColivEase

The best neighbourhoods in Toronto for students are the ones with a direct, reliable transit link to your campus: the Annex, Harbord Village and Kensington-Chinatown for U of T St. George and OCAD; Church-Wellesley, Cabbagetown and St. Lawrence for TMU and George Brown; and the areas along Line 1, Line 6 or local bus routes near York, Humber, Seneca and the Scarborough campuses. If downtown rent stretches your budget, sharing a place a few subway stops further out usually saves more money than it costs in commute time.

Key takeaways

  • Start from your campus, not the map. A 25-minute ride with no transfers beats a "closer" place that needs two buses.
  • Downtown campuses (U of T St. George, TMU, OCAD, George Brown) are walkable from several central neighbourhoods, but rooms there tend to cost the most.
  • Suburban campuses (York, UTSC, UTM, Humber, Seneca, Centennial) are easier and usually cheaper to live near, but they lean heavily on buses and LRT lines.
  • Sharing is the norm. U of T's own data puts the average room in Toronto at about $1,150 to $1,220 a month, well below a one-bedroom.
  • Check the basics before signing: rooming-house licence, legal basement unit, and a real landlord you've verified.

How to choose a student neighbourhood

Three filters do most of the work:

  1. Commute. Look at the full door-to-door trip at the time your classes start, including walking and transfers. The TTC trip planner is more honest than "10 minutes to campus" in a listing.
  2. Budget. Rent is only part of it. Utilities, internet and a transit pass can add a lot, so compare all-in costs. Our guide to the cost of renting a room in Toronto breaks this down.
  3. Vibe. Do you want late-night food and live music, or a quiet street where you can sleep before an 8 a.m. lab? Visit at night and on a weekend if you can.

Downtown campuses

U of T St. George

The Annex is the classic choice: Victorian houses split into flats and rooms, bookstores, cafés along Bloor, and stations at Spadina, Bathurst and St. George. Harbord Village and the streets around Kensington Market and Chinatown are a short walk to most buildings. Koreatown and Christie Pits are a little further west on Line 2 and usually a touch cheaper.

If prices are too high, look along Line 2 to the west or along Line 1 north. The U of T Off-Campus Housing Finder, open to students with a UTORid, lists rooms and has a roommate search.

Toronto Metropolitan University (TMU)

TMU sits at Yonge and Gould, right next to the renamed TMU station (formerly Dundas) and College station. Church-Wellesley Village and the Garden District are walkable, and Cabbagetown and St. Lawrence offer older streets with more character. The Danforth area along Line 2 is a solid alternative: ride to Bloor-Yonge and transfer south.

Expect noise and crowds around Yonge-Dundas. Many listings are small condo units, so check closet space and whether a "bedroom" actually has a window.

OCAD University

OCAD's main building on McCaul Street is surrounded by Grange Park, Chinatown and Kensington Market, all walkable. Queen West is close and creative but loud on weekend nights. Osgoode and St Patrick stations on Line 1 make it easy to live further out on the subway.

George Brown Polytechnic

George Brown (renamed from George Brown College in 2025) has several campuses, so check which one you'll attend most. For St. James near King and Jarvis, St. Lawrence and Corktown are walkable. The Waterfront campus is near Queens Quay East, a walk or short streetcar ride from Union Station. For the Casa Loma campus, the Annex and Seaton Village work well, with Dupont station on Line 1 nearby.

Suburban campuses

York University (Keele campus)

York University and Pioneer Village stations on Line 1 put the campus on the subway, so anywhere along the Spadina side of Line 1 is fair game, including Downsview and areas around Sheppard West. Line 6 Finch West, which opened in December 2025, connects to Line 1 at Finch West station and serves the corridor to the west.

Many houses right around campus rent rooms individually to students. That can be affordable and social, but check that a house with many separately rented rooms is properly licensed.

U of T Scarborough and Centennial (Progress campus)

Line 3 Scarborough closed permanently in 2023 and was replaced by buses, so Line 2 now ends at Kennedy station. Both campuses are reached by bus. Living close by in Highland Creek, Morningside or around Scarborough Centre usually means a much shorter, more predictable commute than coming from downtown. These are quieter, residential areas with good food options, especially Asian restaurants and plazas.

U of T Mississauga

UTM is in Mississauga, outside the TTC system, and served by MiWay buses, with connections to Line 2 at Kipling station. Most students live in Erin Mills or central Mississauga. If you'll be in Toronto often for work or friends, factor in the extra fare and travel time.

Humber Polytechnic and Seneca Polytechnic

For Humber's North campus, Line 6 Finch West now runs to Humber College station, which makes living along Finch West or near Finch West station (Line 1) far more practical than before. Humber's Lakeshore campus is in south Etobicoke near Mimico and Long Branch, served by the Queen streetcar and the Lakeshore West GO line.

Seneca's Newnham campus is near Finch Avenue East and Highway 404. Students often live around Don Mills station (Line 4) or Finch station (Line 1) and take a bus. Seneca also has a campus on York's Keele campus, so check your timetable first.

What to watch out for anywhere in the city

  • Basement units. Many affordable rooms are in basements. Look for proper windows, a second exit and working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.
  • Rooming houses. Since March 31, 2024, multi-tenant houses in Toronto need a City licence, and operators must post it at the entrance.
  • Scams. Rental scams spike before each term. Never send money before you've verified the landlord and seen the unit in person or on a live video call. Read our guide to Toronto rental scams.
  • Transit gaps. A place near "a bus" is not the same as a place near frequent service. Check how often your route runs in the evening and on weekends.
  • Late-summer rush. Demand peaks before September. If you're coming from abroad, see our international student renting checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Where do most U of T St. George students live off campus?

Many live within walking distance in the Annex, Harbord Village and Kensington-Chinatown, or a short subway ride away along Line 1 and Line 2. Students on a tighter budget often share a house or apartment further out, such as along Line 2 west or in North York, and accept a longer ride.

Is it better to live downtown or in North York as a student?

Downtown wins on walkability and short commutes if your campus is downtown, but rooms usually cost more. North York along Line 1 tends to offer more space for the money and a direct subway ride, so it suits students at York, Seneca or anyone willing to commute about half an hour each way.

What is the cheapest area in Toronto for students?

There is no single cheapest area, and prices change street by street. In general, rooms in shared homes further from downtown, but still near a subway or LRT stop, cost less than downtown units. Compare the full cost including transit time before deciding.

Is Scarborough a good place to live for U of T Scarborough or Centennial students?

Yes, living near campus in Scarborough usually means a much shorter commute, since both campuses rely on buses rather than the subway. Areas like Highland Creek, Morningside and around Scarborough Centre are popular with students. Check bus frequency to campus before you sign.

How do I check if a student rooming house is legal in Toronto?

Since March 31, 2024, operators of multi-tenant (rooming) houses in Toronto need a City licence, and they must post the licence at the entrance. Ask to see it, and be cautious if a landlord renting many rooms separately cannot show one.
