Ontario Trillium Benefit for Renters: Claim Your Rent

Ontario Trillium Benefit rent guide: who qualifies, how to claim rent on taxes with form ON-BEN, which rent receipts to keep, and tips for roommates.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

Ontario doesn't let renters deduct rent directly, but rent you pay can earn you money back through the Ontario Trillium Benefit (OTB). You claim it by filing your income tax and benefit return and completing form ON-BEN, where you enter the rent you paid for your home in Ontario. The CRA then calculates your credit and pays it, usually monthly, starting the July after you file.

This is general information, not tax advice. For your situation, check with the CRA or a free tax clinic.

Key takeaways

  • Rent isn't a deduction in Ontario, but it counts toward a credit. The Ontario energy and property tax credit (OEPTC), part of the OTB, uses the rent you paid.
  • You apply through your tax return. Fill in form ON-BEN with your rent, address and landlord details. Tax software includes it.
  • File every year, even with little or no income. No return, no benefit.
  • Roommates claim only their own share of the rent.
  • Keep rent receipts or payment records for six years. You don't send them in, but the CRA can ask.

What the Ontario Trillium Benefit is

The OTB combines three Ontario credits into one payment:

CreditWhat it's forDoes rent matter?
Ontario energy and property tax credit (OEPTC)Helps with property tax and energy costs; renters qualify through rentYes, rent is part of the calculation
Northern Ontario energy credit (NOEC)Extra help for people living in Northern OntarioApplies only in the North, so it won't apply to most GTA renters
Ontario sales tax credit (OSTC)Helps with sales tax costs for low- and modest-income residentsNo, it's based on income and family

For renters in Toronto and the GTA, the OEPTC is the part tied to rent. The amount depends on what you paid for your home, your family situation and your family's net income, and it shrinks as income rises. Because the rules and maximums are set each year, we haven't listed dollar amounts here. Your CRA notice shows exactly what you'll get.

Who qualifies for the Ontario Trillium Benefit on rent

In general, you may be able to get the energy and property tax credit for a benefit year if:

  • you were a resident of Ontario on December 31 of the tax year, and
  • you paid rent (or property tax) for your principal residence in Ontario for that year, or lived in a designated student residence, a long-term care home or on a reserve, and
  • you're 18 or older, or you have (or had) a spouse or common-law partner, or you're a parent who lives with your child.

"Principal residence" means the place you actually live. Rent for a cottage, a storage unit or a room you rented for a friend doesn't count.

Newcomers and international students

Arrived partway through the year? You can still apply if you meet the conditions, by filing a return for the year you became a resident of Canada for tax purposes. Many international students are considered residents for tax purposes, but it depends on your ties to Canada, so check the CRA's residency guidance or ask a clinic. For more on settling in as a student, see our guide to international student housing in Toronto.

How to claim rent on taxes in Ontario

Step 1: Add up the rent you paid

Total the rent you paid for each Ontario address you lived in during the tax year. If you moved in September, include your old place for January to August and your new place for September to December.

What to include and leave out:

  • Include the rent paid by you, or for you (for example, by a parent covering your rent), for your home.
  • Leave out the portion your roommates paid, even if the whole rent went through your account.
  • Shared housing: if you split rent, enter only your share. Our guide to splitting rent fairly helps you keep the split clear from day one.
  • Subsidized housing: the CRA suggests checking with your landlord whether property tax was paid for your unit, since that affects whether you can claim.

Step 2: Complete form ON-BEN

ON-BEN is the "Application for the Ontario Trillium Benefit and the Ontario Senior Homeowners' Property Tax Grant." Most tax software asks you a few questions and fills it in for you. Expect to provide:

  • the address of each Ontario home you rented
  • the rent you paid for each
  • the name of your landlord (or whoever you paid)
  • whether you lived in a designated student residence

The sales tax credit doesn't need any extra form. Filing your return is enough.

Step 3: File on time and watch your notice

File by the regular tax deadline so payments start on time in July. Your notice of assessment, and your CRA My Account, show your OTB amount and payment schedule. Payments from July 2026 to June 2027 are based on 2025 returns; your 2026 return, filed in spring 2027, sets the next year's payments.

Do I need rent receipts?

You don't attach receipts when you file, but the CRA can ask you to prove the rent you claimed, sometimes long after you've moved. The CRA generally expects you to keep supporting records for six years.

Good proof includes:

  • Rent receipts showing the address, your name, the amount, the period covered and the landlord's name and signature
  • Bank records: e-transfers, cheques or pre-authorized payments to your landlord
  • Your lease, showing the monthly rent and your name
  • A note of your share, if you split rent with roommates

Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act requires a landlord to give a tenant a rent receipt free of charge on request. Ask once a year in writing, or after you move out, so you're never scrambling later.

Paying cash? Protect yourself

Cash leaves no record. If your landlord insists on cash, ask for a signed receipt every time. Better still, pay by e-transfer or cheque so your bank keeps a record for you. Living with your landlord, or in a room where the owner also lives? Rent you pay for your home generally still counts for the OEPTC, so keep your records just the same.

Students living in residence

If you lived in a designated university or college residence in Ontario, you don't enter rent. Instead, ON-BEN asks whether you lived in a student residence, and the credit is calculated with a fixed residence amount. That usually makes the energy and property tax credit much smaller than for a student renting off campus, but it's still worth applying, and the sales tax credit is separate.

Moving off campus mid-year? Claim the rent for your off-campus months and indicate the residence months too.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Not filing because you earned nothing. Students and newcomers often skip a return and miss the OTB entirely.
  • Claiming the whole rent when you share. Each roommate claims only what they paid.
  • Forgetting an address. Every Ontario home you rented during the year counts.
  • Losing proof. Without receipts or bank records, a CRA review gets stressful.
  • Not updating your address and banking details with the CRA after you move, which can delay payments.

Where to get free help

The CRA's Community Volunteer Income Tax Program runs free tax clinics for people with modest income and a simple tax situation, including many students and newcomers. Libraries, settlement agencies and campus student unions in the GTA often host them during tax season.

Frequently asked questions

Can roommates both claim rent for the Ontario Trillium Benefit?

Yes, but each person claims only their own share of the rent they actually paid. If three of you split $3,000 a month equally, each person reports their $1,000 share, not the full amount. Keep your own proof of payment, such as e-transfer records or receipts in your name.

Can students living in residence get the Ontario Trillium Benefit?

Students who lived in a designated university, college or private school residence in Ontario can still apply on form ON-BEN by indicating that they lived in a student residence instead of entering rent. The energy and property tax credit for residence students is calculated differently and is usually much smaller than for students who rent off campus. The sales tax credit part of the benefit doesn't depend on rent.

When is the Ontario Trillium Benefit paid?

The CRA pays the benefit for Ontario, usually monthly, over a benefit year that runs from July to the following June. Payments from July 2026 to June 2027 are based on your 2025 tax return. If your yearly amount is small, it may be paid as a single lump sum instead, so check your CRA notice.

What if I forgot to claim rent on my taxes in a past year?

You may be able to ask the CRA to adjust a past return to add your ON-BEN information. Gather your rent records for that year first. A free tax clinic or the CRA can tell you how far back you can go and how to submit the change.

Does my landlord have to give me a rent receipt in Ontario?

Yes. Under Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act, a landlord must give a tenant a receipt for rent free of charge when asked, and a former tenant can request one for up to 12 months after the tenancy ends. Ask in writing so you have a record of the request.
