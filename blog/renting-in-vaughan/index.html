Vaughan Rentals: VMC Condos, Areas and Commutes

Searching Vaughan rentals? Compare VMC condos, Woodbridge, Maple and Thornhill, see how Line 1, Viva and GO connect, and what to check before signing a lease.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

Vaughan rentals fall into two very different worlds: new high-rise condos around Vaughan Metropolitan Centre (VMC), where Line 1 ends, and suburban houses in Woodbridge, Maple, Concord and Thornhill that rent rooms, basements and whole homes. If you commute to York University or downtown by subway, VMC is the obvious starting point. If you want more space, a quieter street or a lower cost per person, a room or basement in a nearby house is usually worth comparing.

Here's how the city is laid out for renters, how transit really connects, and what to check before you hand over any money.

Key takeaways

  • VMC is the transit anchor. Line 1, the Viva Orange rapidway on Highway 7 and the YRT bus terminal all meet there.
  • York University is close by subway. It's three stops south of VMC on Line 1.
  • Outside VMC, Vaughan is car-friendly first. Check bus frequency from the exact address if you don't drive.
  • Most non-condo rentals are in houses. Rooms, basements and whole homes shared by roommates.
  • Secondary suites have rules. Vaughan permits them only in certain house types and they must meet zoning, Building Code and Fire Code requirements.

Getting around Vaughan

  • TTC Line 1 runs north through York University to Vaughan Metropolitan Centre at Highway 7 and Jane. The extension opened in December 2017, so you can ride straight to downtown without a transfer.
  • YRT and Viva. York Region Transit runs local buses across Vaughan, and Viva Orange runs along the Highway 7 rapidway in dedicated lanes. At VMC, the Viva platforms and the SmartVMC bus terminal connect to the subway through underground passages.
  • GO Transit. Maple and Rutherford stations on the Barrie line serve central Vaughan, with trains toward Union Station.
  • One Fare. Since February 2024, transfers between YRT and the TTC (and GO) are free within the transfer window when you tap PRESTO, debit or credit, so a bus-plus-subway commute no longer means paying twice.

Vaughan to York University commute

For students, VMC is about as convenient as it gets outside the campus itself: Line 1 runs VMC, Highway 407, Pioneer Village, York University, so it's a short ride with no transfer. Living elsewhere in Vaughan, you would typically take a YRT or Viva bus to VMC or Pioneer Village first. Map the trip at your real class times before you sign, because bus frequency drops evenings and weekends on some routes. Vaughan Metropolitan Centre condos

VMC is Vaughan's new downtown, built up around the subway station with towers, offices, a transit hub and parks still taking shape.

Who it suits: York University students, commuters along Line 1, and young professionals who want amenities like a gym, concierge and parcel room.

What to expect when renting a VMC condo:

  • Building rules apply to you. As a tenant you must follow the condo's declaration, by-laws and rules, including move-in elevator bookings, amenity hours and guest parking.
  • Ask what's included. Some units come with parking or a locker, some don't. Ask who pays hydro and water, since many newer condos meter them separately.
  • Shared condos are common. Two-bedroom units are often rented by two roommates or by a tenant who rents out the second room. Ask whether everyone will be on the lease.
  • Construction is part of the scene. VMC is still being built, so expect cranes and some noise nearby.

Types of Vaughan rentals

  • Condo units around VMC and along Highway 7, often rented whole or shared by two roommates.
  • Rooms in houses, sometimes in owner-occupied homes. If you share a kitchen or bathroom with the owner or their close family, the Residential Tenancies Act generally doesn't apply, so your written agreement matters more.
  • Basement apartments. Vaughan allows secondary suites in specific detached, semi-detached and street townhouses, as long as they meet zoning, the Building Code and the Fire Code.
  • Whole houses rented by a group of roommates, sometimes room by room.

Vaughan or closer to the core?

If you're torn between Vaughan and North York or downtown, compare three things: rent and space (house rooms in Vaughan are often bigger and may include parking), transport costs (a VMC renter who rides the subway pays much the same as a Toronto renter, but a car in Woodbridge adds insurance, gas and parking), and time (Highway 400 and 407 traffic at rush hour can make driving slower than the subway). Check both options at your real travel times.

What to watch for before you sign

  • Confirm who you are renting from. Owner, registered agent or tenant subletting. If an agent is involved, check them on RECO's public register.
  • Get a written lease. Most residential tenancies in Ontario use the standard lease form.
  • Know the deposit rules. In covered tenancies, a landlord can take a last month's rent deposit and a refundable key or fob deposit, not a separate damage deposit.
  • For basements, check safety. Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, a window or door you could escape through, and adequate ceiling height.
  • For condos, get the rules. Ask for a copy of the building rules and the move-in procedure before move-in day.
  • Beware of too-good deals. Never pay a deposit before viewing the actual unit and seeing a lease.

This is general information, not legal advice. For questions about your rights, contact the Landlord and Tenant Board or a community legal clinic.

Frequently asked questions

How do I commute from Vaughan to York University?

From Vaughan Metropolitan Centre, take Line 1 south: York University station is three stops away, after Highway 407 and Pioneer Village. If you live elsewhere in Vaughan, a YRT or Viva bus to VMC or Pioneer Village usually gets you onto the subway. With PRESTO, debit or credit, the One Fare Program makes YRT-to-TTC transfers free within the transfer window.

Is renting a condo at Vaughan Metropolitan Centre worth it?

It suits people who want new buildings, amenities and a subway station at the door, especially commuters to York University or downtown along Line 1. You trade a quieter suburban street for a busy, still-growing district. Compare total monthly cost, including parking and utilities, against a room in a nearby house.

Can I live in Vaughan without a car?

Around VMC and along the Highway 7 Viva corridor, yes for most daily needs. In much of Woodbridge, Maple and Kleinburg, life is more car-oriented and buses run less often, so check routes and frequencies from the exact address.

Are basement apartments legal in Vaughan?

Vaughan permits secondary suites in certain detached, semi-detached and street townhouses, as long as they meet the zoning by-law, the Ontario Building Code and the Fire Code. Ask the landlord whether the unit was built with a permit, and look for safety basics like alarms and a proper exit.

Which GO stations are in Vaughan?

Maple and Rutherford stations on the Barrie line are in Vaughan. They are handy if you work downtown near Union, but check the schedule for your hours, since service levels vary through the day.
