International Student Housing in Toronto: 2026 Checklist

International student housing in Toronto, step by step: when to start, residence vs off-campus, documents, remote viewings, the lease, deposits and scams.

Updated November 3, 2026 · 9 min read · ColivEase

International students looking for housing in Toronto should start with their school's housing office, apply for residence as soon as they accept an offer, and begin the off-campus search about one to two months before move-in, when most listings appear. Never send money for a place you haven't verified, make sure you sign Ontario's Standard Lease where it applies, and don't pay more than the deposits Ontario allows.

Key takeaways

  • Residence deadlines come early. At U of T, for example, first-year students had to apply through the residence portal by March 31 to keep the residence guarantee for 2026 entry.
  • Off-campus listings appear late. Many tenants give 60 days' notice, so most units hit the market one to two months ahead.
  • Prepare documents now: passport, study permit or letter of introduction, proof of enrolment and proof of funds.
  • Verify before you pay. Live video tours and a trusted local contact beat any promise made over chat.
  • Know the deposit limits: a last month's rent deposit of up to one month's rent and a refundable key deposit. No damage deposits.
  • Use your school. Housing offices can help you check listings, leases and red flags for free.

If you can't lock in a long-term place before you fly, book a short-term stay for your first few weeks. Viewing in person after you land is the single best protection against scams.

Documents to have ready

Keep digital copies in one folder so you can apply quickly:

  • Passport (share a copy only once the landlord is verified)
  • Study permit, or your port-of-entry letter of introduction if you haven't arrived yet
  • Letter of acceptance or proof of enrolment
  • Proof of funds: recent bank statements, scholarship letters or a sponsor's letter
  • References: a previous landlord, a professor or an employer, if you have them
  • Guarantor information, if a landlord asks and you have someone in Canada who can help

You don't need a Social Insurance Number to rent. Service Canada lists renting a property and filling out a rental application among situations where you don't have to provide it. If you have no Canadian credit history yet, that's normal for students: offer proof of funds, your enrolment letter and references instead, and remember that a lack of credit history shouldn't be held against you in Ontario.

Viewing a place remotely, safely

If you're still abroad:

  1. Ask for a live video tour, not pre-recorded clips. Ask the person to open closets, run the taps, show the view from the window and walk to the front door so you can see the street.
  2. Confirm who they are. Ask for their full name and check that they own or manage the unit. A property management company should have a website, office address and phone number you can verify.
  3. Ask a trusted friend in Toronto to view the unit in person if possible.
  4. Search the address and photos online. Scammers often copy photos from real listings.
  5. Ask your school's housing office to look at the listing and lease if anything feels off.

Signing the Standard Lease

Most residential tenancies in Ontario must use the government's Standard Lease form. It sets out the rent, what's included, deposits and the rules both sides must follow. Read the "additional terms" section carefully, because a term that breaks the Residential Tenancies Act can't be enforced, even if you signed it.

If your landlord doesn't give you the Standard Lease, you can ask for it in writing. If they don't provide it within 21 days, Ontario's rules give you options, including withholding up to one month's rent in some circumstances. Our full guide to the Ontario Standard Lease walks through each section.

One important exception: if you share a kitchen or bathroom with the owner or the owner's close family, the Residential Tenancies Act generally doesn't apply, and neither does the Standard Lease. That's common in homestays and some room rentals, so ask before you sign.

Deposits and payments

In Ontario, a landlord can collect:

  • A rent deposit of no more than one month's rent, which must be used for your last month
  • A refundable key deposit

Damage deposits are not allowed. The landlord owes you annual interest on the rent deposit at the rent increase guideline rate (2.1% for 2026). A landlord can't make several months of prepaid rent a condition of renting to you; if you choose to offer it, do so only after verifying the landlord and signing the lease, and get a receipt.

Pay by a traceable method, such as an e-transfer to a verified landlord or a cheque, and keep every receipt and message.

Scams that target international students

Students are targeted because they often rent from abroad, under time pressure. In June 2026, Toronto police announced an arrest in an alleged rental fraud near Yonge and Finch in which international students paid deposits for apartments they hadn't viewed. Watch for:

  • Too-good-to-be-true rent in popular areas
  • "I'm overseas, I'll mail you the keys" stories
  • The same unit rented to several people, each paying a deposit
  • Fake agents asking for passport and permit copies before any viewing
  • Pressure to pay today because "many students are interested"
  • Sublets from someone with no right to sublet

If you're targeted, contact your bank immediately, report to the Toronto Police Service and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, and tell your school's housing office. Read more in our guide to Toronto rental scams.

Use your school's housing office

  • University of Toronto: the Off-Campus Housing Finder is open to students with a UTORid and includes a roommate finder.
  • Toronto Metropolitan University: the Off-Campus Housing Office offers appointments and resources for current and incoming students.
  • York University: off-campus housing services include a listing platform and support for tenant-landlord issues.
  • Colleges such as Humber, Seneca, Centennial and George Brown have housing or student-life teams who can help.

These services are free, and staff have seen every scam in the book.

After you move in

A few small steps in your first week can save you a lot of trouble later:

  • Document the unit's condition. Take dated photos and videos of every room, including any damage, and email them to your landlord so there's a record.
  • Get tenant insurance if you haven't already. It covers your belongings and your liability if, for example, you accidentally cause a leak.
  • Set up utilities and internet that aren't included in your rent, and confirm whose name each account is in.
  • Save your landlord's contact details and the process for repair requests, preferably in writing.
  • Pay rent on time and keep receipts. A clean rental record here becomes a reference for your next place.

Frequently asked questions

When should international students start looking for housing in Toronto?

Apply for residence as soon as you accept your offer, since deadlines can come early in the year. For off-campus rentals, most units are listed about one to two months before they're available, partly because tenants on monthly leases must give 60 days' notice to move out. Start researching neighbourhoods and budgets earlier, and search actively six to eight weeks before your move.

Can I rent an apartment in Toronto before I arrive in Canada?

Yes, but it's riskier. If you sign from abroad, verify the landlord's identity, get a live video tour, ask someone you trust in Toronto to view it in person, and only pay after you've signed a written lease. Many students book a short-term stay for the first few weeks and sign a long-term lease after viewing in person.

What documents do international students need to rent in Toronto?

Landlords usually ask for a passport, your study permit or letter of introduction, proof of enrolment, proof of funds such as bank statements, and references if you have them. Some also ask for a guarantor. You don't need to give your Social Insurance Number to rent.

Is it safe to pay a deposit before seeing a place?

It's one of the most common ways students lose money. Never pay before you've verified that the person owns or manages the unit and you've seen it in person or on a live video call. Pay by a traceable method and get a receipt.

Does the Ontario Standard Lease apply if I rent a room?

Usually yes, if the Residential Tenancies Act applies to your rental. A key exception is when you share a kitchen or bathroom with the owner or the owner's close family; then the Act and the Standard Lease generally don't apply, and you have fewer protections.

Where can international students get help with housing in Toronto?

Start with your school's housing office. U of T has an Off-Campus Housing Finder, TMU has an Off-Campus Housing Office, and York has off-campus housing services. Colleges also have housing or student-life staff who can review listings and leases with you.
