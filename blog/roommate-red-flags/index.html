Roommate Red Flags: Warning Signs Before You Move In

Roommate red flags to spot before you move in: scam warning signs, behaviour that predicts trouble, and lease set-ups that leave you exposed in Ontario.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

Roommate red flags fall into three groups: scam signals (money or documents requested before you can verify anything), behaviour that predicts conflict (vagueness, disrespect, pressure), and lease set-ups that leave you without protection. One scam signal is enough to walk away; behavioural and lease red flags are reasons to slow down and ask more questions. Spotting them early is far easier than untangling a bad living situation later.

Below is a deeper checklist than the quick list in our guide to finding a roommate in Toronto, with what each flag usually means and what to do about it.

Key takeaways

  • Money before verification is the brightest red flag. No deposits, "holding fees" or transfers before a viewing and a written agreement.
  • Watch how people handle questions. Defensiveness in a 30-minute chat becomes stonewalling at month three.
  • Check the paper trail. Who is on the lease, whether the landlord knows about you, and what you'll sign.
  • Judge behaviour, not background. Culture, accent, religion or age tell you nothing about whether someone pays rent on time.
  • Trust the pattern. One small flag is normal; three together is a decision.

Roommate scam warning signs

These are deal-breakers. Scammers target busy seasons, newcomers and students who are searching from abroad.

Money and payment red flags

  1. A deposit before you've seen the room. A "roommate" who wants your share of first and last month's rent, or a "holding fee," before any viewing.
  2. The overpayment trick. They send a cheque or transfer for more than they owe and ask you to send back the difference. The original payment later bounces or is reversed.
  3. Unusual payment methods. Gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers abroad or payment to a third party "handling the booking."
  4. Rent far below the area. A private room in a prime location at a price nothing else comes close to.

Identity and verification red flags

  1. No live video, no in-person meeting. Constant excuses: travelling, bad connection, camera broken.
  2. Requests for your SIN, passport scans or banking logins before you've met. A roommate never needs your SIN.
  3. A story that shifts. The unit, the landlord's name or the reason they're overseas changes between messages.
  4. Copied photos. The room pictures appear on other listings or hotel sites when you reverse-search them.
  5. They can't show they have the right to rent the room. No lease, no landlord contact, no consent to sublet.

If you're unsure, our pillar on rental scams in Toronto explains how to verify a landlord or agent and what to do if you've already paid.

Signs of a bad roommate: behaviour to watch

These aren't crimes, but they reliably predict friction. Use your first video call and in-person meeting to look for them. Our list of questions to ask a roommate gives you the conversation starters.

During the conversation

  1. Vague about money. "I'll figure it out" when you ask how they'll cover rent between jobs or terms.
  2. Every past roommate was "crazy." One bad experience is normal; a string of villains is a pattern.
  3. Dismissive of your questions. Eye-rolling at "How do you split cleaning?" signals how real disagreements will go.
  4. Oversells being easygoing. "I'm chill about everything" often means they haven't thought about it, or don't want to commit to anything.
  5. Pushes boundaries already. A partner who will "only stay a few nights a week," or a pet they mention at the end.
  6. Pressure to decide today. Legitimate roommates want a good fit too.

What you can observe

  1. The state of their current place on a live video call, compared with how they describe themselves.
  2. How they talk about their current landlord or roommates in front of you.
  3. Punctuality and follow-through. Late to every call and slow to send what they promised is a preview of bill payments.
  4. Reluctance to give a reference from a previous landlord or roommate.

Red flag or just different?

Not every difference is a red flag. A night-shift nurse and a 9-to-5 student can live together happily if they agree on noise. Ask whether the difference can be handled with a clear rule. If yes, it's a compatibility question. If they refuse to discuss a rule at all, that refusal is the red flag.

What to do when you spot a red flag

  • For a scam signal: stop, don't send money or documents, and report it to the platform and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
  • For a behavioural flag: ask a follow-up question and see how they respond. Openness is a good sign; defensiveness confirms the flag.
  • For a lease flag: ask for the document or confirmation in writing. If they won't provide it, keep looking.

Frequently asked questions

What are the biggest roommate red flags?

The most serious are money requests before you have seen the room or signed anything, refusing a live video call or in-person meeting, and a story that keeps changing. After that, watch for vagueness about how they will pay rent, a history of fights with every past roommate, and resistance to putting anything in writing.

What are the signs of a bad roommate during the first meeting?

Pay attention to how they treat your questions. Someone who dismisses your concerns, interrupts, oversells how easygoing they are, or won't give concrete answers about cleaning, guests and money is showing you how future disagreements will go. Disrespect toward a current roommate or landlord in front of you is another warning sign.

How do roommate scams usually work?

Common versions include a 'roommate' who is abroad and wants a deposit before you can view, someone who sends a cheque for more than they owe and asks you to send back the difference, and people who collect deposits for a room they have no right to rent. They all depend on getting money or personal documents from you before you can verify anything.

Should I move in if the roommate won't put me on the lease?

Not without understanding the risks. If you're not on the lease you are usually an occupant, with few rights against the landlord and generally no access to the Landlord and Tenant Board. At minimum, see the head lease, confirm the landlord knows you will live there, and sign a written roommate agreement.

Is it a red flag if a roommate asks for my SIN or passport?

Yes, at the screening stage. A future roommate has no need for your Social Insurance Number, and passport or bank details should never be sent to someone you haven't met. Share only what is needed once you have seen the place and a landlord's application is genuinely in progress.
