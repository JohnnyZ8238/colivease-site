Roommate Moving Out Ontario: Lease, Rent and Deposit

Roommate moving out in Ontario? What happens to a shared lease, who pays rent when a roommate leaves, how to replace them and what to do with the deposit.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

Roommate moving out in Ontario? What happens next depends on one question: is your roommate a co-tenant on the lease, or do they rent a room from you? A co-tenant's notice generally doesn't end the tenancy for the people who stay, the landlord still expects the full rent, and the cleanest way to bring in someone new is usually an assignment with the landlord's written consent.

This guide goes beyond the basics of giving notice (our N9 form guide covers that) and focuses on the messy middle: the person leaving, the people staying, the money and the paperwork.

Key takeaways

  • Check the lease first. Whose names are on it decides who owes the landlord what.
  • One co-tenant's N9 usually doesn't end the tenancy for the roommates who stay.
  • The landlord expects the whole rent. Remaining co-tenants should keep paying in full and sort out the shortfall separately.
  • Swap roommates through an assignment or a new lease, with written landlord consent.
  • The deposit normally stays with the landlord until the tenancy ends; the departing roommate's share is settled between roommates.
  • Roommate disputes over money usually go to Small Claims Court, not the Landlord and Tenant Board (LTB).

This is general information, not legal advice. For your situation, contact the Landlord and Tenant Board or a community legal clinic.

The LTB's Interpretation Guideline 21 notes that the Residential Tenancies Act (RTA) doesn't define "roommate." Someone who lives in the unit but isn't a tenant is often treated as an occupant, with no agreement with the landlord, even if they pay rent to the head tenant. The facts matter, so if your setup is unusual, get advice.

Co-tenant leaving the lease: what actually happens

When a co-tenant wants out, they'll often hand the landlord an N9. The LTB's own brochure says that in a joint tenancy, if the other tenants don't sign the notice, the tenancy continues for them. In practice:

  • The people staying keep their home. The landlord can't treat one roommate's N9 as an eviction of everyone else.
  • The person leaving may not be off the hook. Unless the landlord agrees to release them, or someone properly takes over their spot, they may still be responsible for rent under the lease.
  • Nothing changes on paper by itself. The lease still lists the original names until the landlord signs something new.

If everyone wants to leave

Then all co-tenants sign one N9, with the usual 60 days' notice and a date at the end of a rental period. Mixed plans ("two of us stay, one goes") are where assignment comes in.

Replacing a roommate: the clean way

Option 1: assignment

An assignment transfers a tenant's place in the lease to a new person, who takes over the rights and obligations. Under RTA section 95, a tenant needs the landlord's consent to assign. The landlord can:

  • consent to the assignment,
  • refuse assignment in general, or
  • agree in principle but refuse a particular person. Once they've agreed in principle, they can't arbitrarily or unreasonably refuse a specific person you propose.

If a landlord refuses assignment outright, or doesn't answer a written request within 7 days, the RTA lets the tenant give notice to end the tenancy within 30 days of the request. Think carefully before using that in a shared unit: ending the tenancy can affect everyone who lives there, so talk to your roommates and a legal clinic first.

Option 2: a new lease with the new group

Some landlords prefer to sign a fresh lease with the staying roommates plus the newcomer. That's fine, but read it closely. A new lease shouldn't quietly change your rent or terms in ways the RTA wouldn't otherwise allow; if something looks off, ask a clinic before signing.

Option 3: a sublet (only if they're coming back)

If your roommate is leaving for a term and plans to return, a sublet may fit better. They stay responsible to the landlord while someone else lives there. See our guide to sublets and assignments in Ontario for the consent rules.

Replacement checklist

  • Departing roommate tells everyone in writing, with a move-out date
  • Roommates agree on who looks for the replacement, and how candidates are approved
  • Written request to the landlord to assign (or to sign a new lease), with the candidate's details
  • Landlord's consent in writing, naming the new person
  • Deposit share, keys, fobs and utilities handed over, with receipts
  • New person reads and signs your house agreement

Who pays rent when a roommate leaves?

From the landlord's point of view, rent is owed for the unit, not per bedroom. Most shared leases make co-tenants jointly responsible, so if one share goes missing, the landlord can look to whoever is still there.

The practical rule: pay the full rent on time, then deal with your old roommate. A shortfall can lead to an N4 notice for non-payment against the whole household. After that:

  1. Ask in writing for the missing amount, with dates and your e-transfer history.
  2. Point to your roommate agreement, if you have one, especially any notice period.
  3. Consider Small Claims Court if they won't pay. The LTB generally doesn't handle disputes between roommates.

If your roommate rented a room from you, the result is similar: you owe the landlord the whole rent, and your claim against the roommate is a private one.

What happens to the deposit?

In Ontario, a landlord can collect a rent deposit of no more than one month's rent, and it's applied to the last month of the tenancy. When one roommate leaves mid-lease, the tenancy hasn't ended, so the landlord normally keeps holding the deposit. That surprises many people.

The usual fix is a handover between roommates:

  • The incoming roommate pays the departing roommate their share of the deposit, and both sign a short receipt.
  • Or the remaining roommates reimburse the departing one and collect from the newcomer later.

Ask the landlord to note the change in writing so everyone knows whose money the deposit now represents. Our guide to last month's rent deposit rules covers interest and how the deposit is applied.

When the departing roommate was the head tenant

This is the trickiest case. If the only person on the lease leaves and the roommates were occupants, Guideline 21 says occupants generally must leave when the tenancy ends. Before the head tenant moves out, ask the landlord whether the remaining roommates can take over through an assignment or a new lease, and get the answer in writing. Don't wait until move-out week.

Prevent the next headache

Most of these fights come down to things nobody wrote down. A short roommate agreement that sets a notice period between roommates (30 days is common), who finds the replacement and how the deposit share is handled will save you weeks of stress.

Frequently asked questions

Can my roommate just leave in the middle of the lease?

They can physically move out, but if they're a co-tenant on the lease, walking away doesn't end their obligations by itself. The tenancy usually continues for whoever stays, and the person who left may still be responsible for rent until the landlord releases them or a replacement properly takes over their place.

Do I have to pay my roommate's share of the rent after they move out?

If you're both tenants on the same lease, your landlord will usually expect the full rent for the unit, no matter who paid what. Paying the full amount on time protects your tenancy. You can then try to recover the missing share from your former roommate, which is a separate dispute between you two.

Does the landlord have to accept the replacement roommate I find?

Swapping a roommate on the lease needs the landlord's consent. Under section 95 of the Residential Tenancies Act, once a landlord agrees to assignment in principle, they can't arbitrarily or unreasonably refuse a specific person you propose. A refusal based on a real concern, such as a poor rental history, may be reasonable.

Can I sue my old roommate for unpaid rent at the Landlord and Tenant Board?

Generally no. The LTB deals with disputes between landlords and tenants, not between roommates. Money claims between roommates usually go to Small Claims Court, so keep e-transfer records, messages and your written roommate agreement.

What if my roommate rents a room from me and isn't on the lease?

Then your landlord has no agreement with them; the arrangement is between the two of you. Your house agreement sets the notice they owe you, and you remain responsible to the landlord for the whole rent. If they leave without notice, you'd generally pursue them in Small Claims Court, not at the LTB.
