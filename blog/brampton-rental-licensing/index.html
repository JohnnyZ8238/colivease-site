Brampton Rental Licence: What Tenants Should Check

Brampton rental licence rules as of November 2026: now citywide. How to check if your rental is licensed, basement apartment rules, fines and Caledon's program.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

A Brampton rental licence is now required citywide: as of November 2026, every owner renting out a property with one to four units anywhere in Brampton needs a Residential Rental Licence from the City before advertising, collecting rent or letting anyone move in. The program expanded from five central wards to all ten on January 1, 2026, and runs as a pilot until December 31, 2028. For tenants, the licence is a quick, free way to check that a home has been on the City's radar for safety.

This is general information, not legal advice. For questions about your tenancy, contact the Landlord and Tenant Board or a community legal clinic; for licensing, call Brampton 311.

Key takeaways

  • Citywide since January 1, 2026: wards 2, 6, 8, 9 and 10 joined wards 1, 3, 4, 5 and 7.
  • Who needs it: owners renting properties with one to four residential units, including owner-occupied homes with a rented basement or other additional unit.
  • Free to apply as of the City's December 2025 announcement; licences are renewed every year and don't transfer to a new owner.
  • Escalating fines for renting without a licence: $750, then $1,250, then $1,500 for later offences.
  • Basements must be registered with the City as additional residential units, and the City publishes a list and map of registered units.
  • Caledon launched its own licensing in September 2026, starting with short-term rentals.

How Brampton rental licence rules work

Brampton started its Residential Rental Licensing (RRL) pilot in January 2024 in five central wards, to tackle unsafe and overcrowded rentals. In December 2025, following a deadly fire at a rental home, Council expanded it citywide and added tougher fines.

Under the program, an owner must hold a licence before:

  • advertising a unit for rent,
  • collecting rent, or
  • letting a tenant move in.

The licence must be displayed in a visible spot inside the rental, and the licence number should appear on ads and on documents like leases and receipts. Owners must renew every year, and a buyer has to apply for a new licence after a sale.

Not covered: licensed lodging houses (they fall under separate business licensing) and larger buildings with five or more units.

Is my rental licensed in Brampton?

A few minutes of checking before you sign can save you a lot of trouble.

Your pre-lease checklist

  • Ask for the licence number in writing. A legitimate landlord should have it ready.
  • Look for it on the ad. No number on a Brampton listing is a reason to ask questions.
  • Check the licence on site. It should be posted inside the unit.
  • For a basement or second unit, search the City's registered units list by address.
  • Confirm with the City. If the landlord's answer is vague, contact Brampton 311.
  • Look for the basics: working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, clear exits and no blocked-off windows or hallways.

A missing licence doesn't automatically mean a scam, but combined with pressure to pay a deposit quickly or a refusal to show the unit, it's a serious red flag. Our guide to rental scams in Toronto covers the warning signs that apply across the GTA.

Brampton basement apartment rules

Basements are where most of Brampton's rental questions come up. Here's what tenants should know:

  • Registration: to be legal, a second unit, third unit or garden suite must be registered with the City of Brampton. Registration involves permits and inspections.
  • Licensing: a rented additional unit also needs a Residential Rental Licence, even when the owner lives upstairs.
  • Check the list: the City publishes a map and a list of registered two-unit and three-unit dwellings and garden suites.
  • Safety first: a legal basement unit should have proper exits, adequate windows, working alarms and fire separation from the main house.

If the owner lives in the house and you share a kitchen or bathroom with them, the Residential Tenancies Act usually doesn't apply, which means fewer protections. A self-contained basement with its own kitchen and bathroom is different. For a full rundown of what makes a basement legal and safe, read our pillar guide to legal basement apartments.

Shared houses and students in Brampton

Brampton is home to Sheridan College's Davis campus and Algoma University's Brampton campus, and many international students share houses. City guidance says rooms can be rented without a lodging house licence to up to four lodgers, in up to four bedrooms per dwelling unit. A home renting rooms to more people than that generally needs a lodging house licence.

SetupWhat usually applies
Whole house rented to one group on one leaseResidential Rental Licence (1–4 units)
Up to 4 bedrooms rented to up to 4 lodgers per unitNo lodging house licence needed; ask whether the property also holds an RRL
More than 4 lodgers renting separate roomsLodging house licence under business licensing
Basement unit rented separatelyRegistered additional unit plus Residential Rental Licence

Signs of trouble include bunk beds in living rooms, padlocks on every door, or more than one person per bedroom without your agreement. New to renting in Canada? Our guide to international student housing in Toronto explains leases, deposits and your rights.

What if your rental isn't licensed?

Finding out your landlord has no licence can be unsettling, but it doesn't put you in trouble. The fines are aimed at owners, not tenants. A few sensible steps:

  • Keep paying rent and keep proof of every payment. Withholding rent can give the landlord grounds for an N4.
  • Ask the landlord, in writing, whether they've applied for a licence and when they expect it.
  • Report safety problems right away through Brampton 311, especially missing alarms, blocked exits or overcrowding.
  • Get advice before you move out. A community legal clinic can tell you how your lease and the Residential Tenancies Act apply.
  • Write everything down: dates, photos and messages make any later complaint much stronger.

Caledon rental licensing

Just north of Brampton, the Town of Caledon launched its own Residential Rental Licensing program on September 8, 2026, citing overcrowding, illegal units and poor maintenance. It's rolling out in phases:

  • Short-term rentals (like Airbnb) needed a licence from launch.
  • Long-term rentals are scheduled to follow in 2027.
  • Fees: an introductory $250 until November 6, 2026, then $500.

If you're considering a long-term rental in Caledon, ask the landlord whether they plan to license once the long-term phase begins.

Frequently asked questions

Do I lose my tenant rights if my Brampton landlord isn't licensed?

Generally no. The licence is the landlord's obligation, and the Residential Tenancies Act protects most tenants whether or not the landlord follows City rules. If your unit is unsafe or unlicensed, you can report it to the City through 311 and get advice from a community legal clinic.

Does a Brampton rental licence cost tenants anything?

No. The licence is applied for and held by the property owner, and the City said in December 2025 that new and renewal applications would remain free. A landlord shouldn't pass a licence fee on to you as a separate charge.

Can international students share a house in Brampton legally?

Yes, if it's set up properly. City guidance allows rooms to be rented to up to four lodgers in up to four bedrooms per dwelling unit without a lodging house licence; more than that generally needs a lodging house licence. Rented basement units must also be registered and licensed.

How do I report an illegal rental in Brampton?

Contact Brampton 311 and give the address and what you've seen, such as overcrowding, no smoke alarms or no visible licence. If it's an emergency, such as fire or no exit, call 911 first.

Do long-term rentals in Caledon need a licence yet?

Not yet, as of November 2026. Caledon's program started with short-term rentals on September 8, 2026, and the Town says long-term rentals will follow in 2027. Check the Town's rental licence page before you sign a lease there.
