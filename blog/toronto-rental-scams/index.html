Rental Scams in Toronto: Red Flags and How to Verify

Spot rental scams in Toronto before you pay. Learn the red flags, how to check an agent on RECO and an owner on OnLand, safe payment rules and where to report.

Updated October 31, 2026 · 8 min read · ColivEase

Most rental scams in Toronto follow one pattern: someone who has no right to rent a unit gets you to pay before you've seen it or signed a proper lease. You can avoid nearly all of them with three habits: see the unit in person (or on a live video walk-through), verify the agent on RECO and the owner on Ontario's land registry, and never pay until you have a signed Ontario Standard Lease.

Below are the scams to know, the red flags, a step-by-step verification checklist, and what to do if you've already paid.

Key takeaways

  • No viewing, no lease, no money. This one rule stops most scams.
  • Check the agent on RECO's public registrant search, and call the brokerage yourself.
  • Check the owner through OnLand, Ontario's online land registry, if anything feels off.
  • Know the deposit limits. In most Ontario tenancies a landlord can ask for up to one month's rent as a rent deposit and a refundable key deposit, not a "damage deposit."
  • Pay traceably and get a receipt. Avoid cash, gift cards, crypto and wires to people you haven't met.
  • Report fast. Your bank first, then police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

Red flags checklist

Walk away, or slow right down, if you see any of these:

  • The price is far below similar units in the same neighbourhood
  • The poster can't or won't show the unit in person or on a live video call
  • You're asked to pay anything before viewing or before signing a lease
  • The "landlord" is out of the country and will courier the keys
  • Pressure to decide within hours
  • Payment by gift card, cryptocurrency, wire transfer or to a third party
  • The lease isn't on Ontario's standard form, or there's no written lease at all
  • They ask for a "damage deposit" or several months' rent up front as a condition of renting
  • Emails and messages from an address unrelated to any real brokerage
  • The story keeps changing

How to verify a landlord or agent before you pay

1. See the unit for real

Visit in person. If you genuinely can't, ask for a live video call where the person walks through every room, opens the front door from the hallway, and shows the building entrance and street. Pre-recorded videos are easy to fake.

2. Reverse-search the photos and the address

Run the listing photos through a reverse image search. If they appear on another listing under a different name or city, that's a strong warning sign. Also search the address: if the unit is listed for sale or on a short-term rental site, ask why.

3. Check the agent on RECO

In Ontario, people who trade in real estate, including leasing, must be registered with the Real Estate Council of Ontario. Use RECO's free registrant search to confirm:

  • the person is registered and active,
  • the brokerage they work for, and
  • whether they have any discipline history.

Then contact the brokerage through the phone number or email on its official website, not the one in the listing, and confirm the agent and the listing are real.

4. Check who owns the property

If you're dealing directly with a "landlord," you can check ownership through OnLand, Ontario's online land registry. For a fee, you can get the parcel register for the property, which shows the registered owner. Keep in mind:

  • The owner might be a numbered company, so ask how the person you're dealing with is connected to it.
  • Ask the landlord for photo ID and compare the name.
  • In a condo, the concierge or property management office can often confirm whether a unit is being leased and who manages it.

5. If it's a room or a sublet, see the head lease

Many rooms in Toronto are rented out by an existing tenant rather than the owner. That can be fine, but ask to see their lease and, for a sublet, confirmation that the landlord consented. Our guide to sublet and assignment in Ontario explains how this should work.

6. Get the lease before any money

For most residential tenancies in Ontario, landlords must use the government's standard lease form. Read it, make sure the names, address, rent and dates are right, and sign before paying. See our Ontario standard lease guide for what each section means.

What a legitimate landlord usually looks like

Real landlords and agents vary, but most of them will:

  • Show you the unit, or arrange a live viewing, without asking for money first
  • Use their real name and give you a way to confirm it
  • Put the tenancy on Ontario's standard lease form
  • Accept a traceable payment and give you a receipt
  • Answer questions about the building, repairs and who to contact
  • Give you time to read the lease before you sign

If the person you're dealing with resists most of these, treat that as your answer.

How to pay safely

  • Pay only after you've viewed the unit, verified the landlord or agent, and signed the lease.
  • Know the limits. In most tenancies covered by the Residential Tenancies Act, a landlord can collect a rent deposit of no more than one month's rent, plus a refundable key deposit. Separate "damage" or "security" deposits aren't allowed. More detail in rent deposit rules in Ontario.
  • Use a traceable method (cheque, bank draft or e-transfer to the verified landlord or brokerage), and keep screenshots.
  • Ask for a receipt. Ontario landlords must give you a receipt for rent or deposits free of charge if you ask.
  • Protect your identity. Share only what's needed. Privacy regulators generally advise against giving your Social Insurance Number to a private business unless the law requires it, and a rental application usually doesn't.

Already paid a scammer? Act fast

  1. Call your bank or payment provider immediately. Ask them to stop, recall or flag the payment. Speed matters.
  2. Report to police. In Toronto, report online through the Toronto Police Service or call the non-emergency line at 416-808-2222. Call 911 only if you're in danger.
  3. Report to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre online or at 1-888-495-8501. Your report helps track scam patterns.
  4. Report the listing to the website or app, and warn the group chat where you found it.
  5. If a registered agent was involved, you can file a complaint with RECO.
  6. Keep everything: screenshots, messages, receipts, the listing URL and the scammer's contact details.

If you also handed over ID documents, watch your accounts closely and consider contacting the credit bureaus about a fraud alert.

Extra care for newcomers and students

Scammers often target people who are still abroad and can't view units. If you can, book short-term accommodation for your first few weeks and search in person. Be just as careful in large community chats and social media groups as anywhere else: a familiar language doesn't mean a verified landlord.

This article is general information, not legal advice. For your specific situation, contact the Landlord and Tenant Board or a community legal clinic.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a rental listing in Toronto is a scam?

Be suspicious if the rent is far below similar units, the poster won't show the unit in person or by live video, or they ask for money before you've seen the place and signed a lease. Reverse-search the photos, check any agent on RECO's registrant search, and confirm the person has the right to rent the unit.

How can I check if a real estate agent is real in Ontario?

Anyone trading in real estate in Ontario, including leasing, must be registered with the Real Estate Council of Ontario (RECO). Search their name on RECO's public registrant search, then contact the brokerage listed there directly using contact details you find yourself, not ones the agent gave you.

How can I find out who owns a property in Ontario?

Ontario's land registry records are available online through OnLand, where you can buy a copy of a property's parcel register for a fee. Note that the owner may be a company, and that a legitimate tenant renting out a room may not be the owner, so ask to see their lease too.

Should I pay a deposit before viewing an apartment?

No. Never pay before you have seen the unit, met the person and have a signed lease. In most Ontario tenancies a landlord can only collect a rent deposit of up to one month's rent and a refundable key deposit.

I paid a rental scammer. What should I do?

Call your bank or payment provider right away to try to stop or reverse the payment. Then report it to Toronto Police (online or at 416-808-2222 for non-emergencies) and to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501, and report the listing to the website where you found it.
