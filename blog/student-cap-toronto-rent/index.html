International Student Cap and Toronto Rent: 2026 Update

How the international student cap affected Toronto rent as of November 2026: softer prices near many campuses, why rent near U of T held up, and student tips.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

The international student cap has eased Toronto rent pressure, but less than in many other cities: as of November 2026, fewer new study permits helped cool rents near many Ontario campuses, while rent near the University of Toronto held up because downtown demand comes from far more than students. In practice, students now have more choice and more room to negotiate than in 2022 or 2023, especially near suburban colleges. Toronto as a whole, though, saw asking rents tick up again in October 2026.

Key takeaways

  • Fewer permits: IRCC expects to issue up to 408,000 study permits in 2026, including about 155,000 for new arrivals, roughly 7% below 2025's target.
  • Campus-area rents softened in many cities, according to Rentals.ca's October 2025 analysis, with the steepest declines in Ontario and B.C. markets that relied heavily on international enrolment.
  • Toronto was the exception: rents near U of T rose 7.3% in that analysis, even as citywide rents fell 6.4%.
  • The cap isn't the only cause. CMHC also points to record new rental supply, slower population growth and a softer job market.
  • Toronto's average asking rent rose 1.6% year over year in October 2026 (Rentals.ca and Urbanation), so don't count on prices falling further.

How the international student cap changed Toronto rent

Canada introduced a cap on new study permit applications in 2024 and tightened it again for 2025 and 2026. IRCC's 2026 plan expects up to 408,000 permits issued in total: about 155,000 for newly arriving students and 253,000 extensions for current and returning students. That compares with targets of 437,000 for 2025 and 485,000 for 2024.

Fewer students arriving means fewer people hunting for rooms each August, and the effect shows up in the data:

  • Population: Statistics Canada estimated that Canada's population fell slightly in the first quarter of 2026, the third quarterly decline in a row, driven by a shrinking number of non-permanent residents.
  • Vacancy: CMHC's 2025 Rental Market Report found Toronto's purpose-built vacancy rate reached 3%, the first time at that level since the pandemic, and listed weaker international student demand among the reasons.
  • Campus areas: Rentals.ca reported that renter inquiries (leads) for listings near campuses fell 39%, compared with a 28% drop nationally, in its October 2025 study.

Why causation is tricky

The cap arrived at the same moment as a record wave of new condo and purpose-built rental completions, a softer job market and wider immigration cuts. All of these push rents the same way. So it's fair to say the cap contributed to cooler student rental markets, but not that it single-handedly lowered Toronto rents.

Rent near U of T: why it held up

Rentals.ca's 2025 analysis looked at listings within 1.5 km of main campuses. Near the University of Toronto's downtown campus, average rents rose 7.3%, while Toronto overall fell 6.4%. Rentals.ca put it down to demand from non-student renters in the downtown core, a market where students are only one group among many.

That pattern makes sense. The Annex, Kensington Market and the streets around Spadina and College aren't student enclaves; they're some of Toronto's most sought-after neighbourhoods. When students leave, other renters quickly fill the gap.

What this means if you're heading to U of T St. George:

  • Expect competition for well-priced rooms, especially from May to September.
  • Consider a short subway or streetcar ride. Areas along Line 1 north of Bloor or Line 2 east and west can offer better value with a direct commute.
  • Share a larger unit. Splitting a two- or three-bedroom often costs about the same per person as a single room but comes with more space.

Is it easier to find student housing now?

For most students, yes, somewhat: there are more listings and fewer people competing for them than at the 2022 to 2023 peak. But it depends a lot on where you're looking.

More choice also means you can be pickier. Don't settle for an overcrowded house, a basement without proper exits or a landlord who wants cash before you've seen the room. With more vacancies, legitimate landlords have every reason to treat good tenants well.

Already renting? Use the extra choice

If you're mid-lease, the market doesn't change your rent until renewal, and for most older units any increase is capped by Ontario's guideline (2.1% for 2026). But a softer market can still work for you. If your current place is overcrowded, far from campus or unsafe, compare what similar money buys today; you may be able to move somewhere better at a similar cost. Just give proper written notice before you leave, usually 60 days before the end of a lease or rental period, so you're not paying for two places at once.

A quick checklist before you sign

  • Verify the landlord's identity and that they own or manage the unit.
  • Never send a deposit for a room you haven't seen, in person or on a live video call.
  • Get the Ontario Standard Lease where it applies, and read it before you pay.
  • Ask who else lives there, and whether you'd share a kitchen or bathroom with the owner.
  • Budget for first month's rent plus a last month's deposit; damage deposits aren't allowed in Ontario.

What to watch next

Toronto's overall asking rents rose 1.6% year over year in October 2026, the first annual increase in nearly three years, according to Rentals.ca and Urbanation, as fewer new buildings come to market. If new construction keeps slowing while student numbers stabilize, the extra choice students enjoy today may narrow. IRCC's next levels plan and its 2027 cap allocations will be worth watching.

Frequently asked questions

How many study permits will Canada issue in 2026?

IRCC said in November 2025 that it expects to issue up to 408,000 study permits in 2026: about 155,000 for newly arriving students and 253,000 extensions for students already here. That's about 7% below the 2025 target of 437,000.

Did the student cap cause Toronto rents to fall?

It was one factor among several. CMHC and rent analysts list fewer international students alongside record new rental supply, slower population growth and a weaker job market. It's hard to separate the effects, so be wary of anyone who credits the cap alone.

Do master's and PhD students need a provincial attestation letter in 2026?

IRCC said that as of January 1, 2026, master's and doctoral students at public designated learning institutions don't need a provincial or territorial attestation letter. They still count toward the overall number of permits issued. Always confirm current rules on canada.ca.

Is on-campus residence cheaper than renting off campus in Toronto?

Not always. Residence often bundles a meal plan and furniture, which can cost more than a shared room off campus but saves on setup costs and commuting. Compare the full monthly cost, including food, transit and utilities, before deciding.

When should international students start looking for housing in Toronto?

For a September start, begin seriously in June or July and avoid paying anything before you've verified the landlord. For January intake, November and December are quieter months with more room to negotiate.
