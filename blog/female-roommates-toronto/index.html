Female Roommates Toronto: Women-Only Rooms and Safety

Looking for female roommates in Toronto? When a women-only room is legal under Ontario's Human Rights Code, plus safety tips for meeting roommates and viewings.

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read · ColivEase

Looking for female roommates in Toronto is common and, in many situations, perfectly legal. Ontario's Human Rights Code generally prohibits sex discrimination in housing, but it makes room for women-only homes in specific cases: when you'd share a kitchen or bathroom with a live-in owner or their family, or when all the accommodation in a building (other than the owner's family's) is limited to people of the same sex.

Below: what the law actually says, how to word a listing, and the safety habits that make meeting strangers a lot less nerve-racking.

Key takeaways

  • The default rule: housing can't be offered on discriminatory terms, including because of sex.
  • Exception 1, s. 21(1): sharing a kitchen or bathroom with a live-in owner or their family.
  • Exception 2, s. 21(2): a building where everyone, apart from the owner's family, is the same sex.
  • The same-sex exception covers sex only. It doesn't permit other preferences, such as religion or ethnicity.
  • Safety first: video call, meet in public, verify identity, view in daylight and never pay before seeing the room.

This is general information, not legal advice. For questions about your rights, contact the Human Rights Legal Support Centre or a community legal clinic; for tenancy issues, the Landlord and Tenant Board.

Are women-only rooms legal in Ontario?

Section 2 of the Human Rights Code gives everyone the right to equal treatment in occupying housing, without discrimination because of grounds that include sex. Section 21 then sets out exceptions. Here's how they apply in real life:

SituationCan it be women-only?Why
Room in a house where you share the kitchen or bathroom with the owner or their family, who live thereGenerally yess. 21(1) shared-accommodation exception
Whole house rented room by room, every room limited to womenGenerally yess. 21(2): all accommodation in the building restricted to the same sex
Owner lives in the house, and every other room is rented to womenGenerally yess. 21(2) excludes the owner's family's own space from the count
One room in a unit inside a large mixed apartment or condo buildingUnclears. 21(2) refers to all the accommodation in the building; get advice
Self-contained apartment rented to a single tenantGenerally noNo exception applies; a sex-based refusal can be discrimination

The fine print that trips people up

  • "Sex only" means sex only. The Ontario Human Rights Commission (OHRC) describes s. 21(2) as an exception for the ground of sex. It doesn't let a listing add "Chinese only," "Christian only" or "no international students." Only the broader s. 21(1) live-in-owner exemption reaches further.
  • Live-in owners and the RTA. The s. 21(1) situation usually lines up with section 5(i) of the Residential Tenancies Act, which means the RTA often doesn't apply either. Read our guide to living with your landlord before you rent that kind of room.
  • Harassment rules are separate. The Code also addresses sexual harassment in housing by a landlord, their agent or another occupant of the building (section 7). A women-only listing is about who gets the room; harassment protections are about how you're treated once you live there.

Wording a listing

If an exception clearly fits, describe the household plainly and stick to facts: "women-only household, four bedrooms, two shared bathrooms." Skip extra preferences that aren't about sex. If you're a tenant searching for a roommate in a unit inside a bigger building, consider phrasing it as information about the household ("currently two women live here") and asking the OHRC's resources or a legal clinic if you're unsure.

Women-only or women-friendly?

Not every woman needs a legally women-only home. Many are happy in a mixed household that's respectful and well run: clear guest rules, locks on bedroom doors, and roommates whose habits and schedules fit. Before you narrow your search to "women only," decide what actually makes you feel safe. Often it's the people, the building and the written agreement, more than the label on the listing.

A safe roommate search for women

Before you meet

  • Move the conversation to a video call. A real person can show their face and the room live. Refusing to video call is a reason to stop.
  • Check the details line up. Name, school or job, and photos should be consistent across their profile and messages.
  • Ask who else lives there and who visits. Partners who are "basically always over" change the household.
  • Look up the address. Make sure it's a real residential building and that the listing photos match the street view.

At the viewing

  • Go in daylight, and bring a friend or share your live location with someone.
  • Meet the actual roommates, not just the person renting the room.
  • Check the bedroom door locks and that the entrance, hallways and building feel secure.
  • Trust your instincts. Pressure to decide on the spot, or to come alone at night, is a reason to leave.

Before you pay

  • Never send money before seeing the room and signing a written agreement.
  • Confirm the person can rent it to you. Ask to see the lease, or confirm you're dealing with the owner or a verified agent.
  • Use traceable payment such as e-transfer, and get a receipt for every payment.

Scammers often target women-only listings because demand is high and people feel pressure to grab a "safe" room fast. Our guides to roommate red flags and rental scams in Toronto cover the common scripts.

Safety checklist for move-in day

  • Written roommate agreement signed, including guest and overnight rules
  • Copy of the lease, or the host's agreement, saved somewhere safe
  • Emergency contact shared with a friend or family member
  • Your own key, and the bedroom lock works
  • Notes on who to contact for building issues (landlord, property manager, superintendent)

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if a roommate or landlord sexually harasses me?

If you're in danger, call 911. The Human Rights Code also has rules against sexual harassment in housing, including by a landlord, their agent or another occupant of the building. Keep messages and notes of what happened, and contact the Human Rights Legal Support Centre or a community legal clinic about your options.

Is it okay to ask a potential roommate for ID?

Yes, it's reasonable for both of you to confirm you are who you say you are, for example by showing photo ID on a video call or at the viewing. Share only what's needed: a name and photo match is enough. Never send your SIN, passport scans or banking details to someone you haven't met.

Does a women-only household mean no male guests?

Not automatically. Who rents the rooms and who can visit are separate questions. Agree on guest and overnight rules as a household and write them into your roommate agreement so nobody is surprised later.

Is a women-only room automatically safer?

No. A women-only label tells you who rents the rooms, not who the people are. Scammers know these listings are in demand, so screen the host and roommates just as carefully, and walk away from anyone who pressures you to pay before a viewing.

What if a 'female only' listing turns out to be run by a man I can't meet?

Treat any mismatch between the listing and the person you're dealing with as a warning sign. Ask for a video call with the actual roommates and proof the host can rent the room. If the story keeps changing, stop and report the listing to the platform.
